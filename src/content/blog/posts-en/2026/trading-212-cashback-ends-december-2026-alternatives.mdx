# Trading 212 Ends Its 1.5% Cashback on 3 December: Best Alternatives in Europe

Trading 212 is scrapping the cashback on its debit card. From **3 December 2026** — one week from today — card spending will no longer earn the 1.5% reward that has been one of the most generous flat rates available to European spenders. ([Source: Be Clever With Your Cash, Trading 212 card review](https://becleverwithyourcash.com/trading-212-card-review/))

If you use the card as your everyday spender, the maths of what you lose is simple. The cashback was capped at £15/€15 per month, so the maximum you could earn was €15 a month — **€180 a year** — reached at €1,000 of monthly spending. Spend less and the loss scales down proportionally: at €800 a month you were earning €12 a month, or €144 a year. That money now stops entirely. There is no reduced rate and no replacement programme announced.

This article explains how the cashback actually worked, what remains of the card after 3 December, and which European alternatives are genuinely worth switching to — with the catches stated plainly, because in cashback marketing the headline percentage is rarely the number you actually receive.

## How the Trading 212 Cashback Actually Worked

The 1.5% rate was introduced in **June 2026**, so it has been live for barely six months. ([Source: Investing in the Web, Trading 212 cashback](https://investingintheweb.com/brokers/trading-212-cashback/)) It was never cash paid back to your balance. Instead, the reward was automatically invested into a **Pie** — Trading 212's portfolio tool — so your cashback bought slices of ETFs or stocks rather than landing as spendable money. That made it a forced-investing perk: good for building a small portfolio from everyday spending, useless if you wanted the money back in your account.

Two conditions had to be met for the cashback to pay out at all:

1. **"Invest cashback" switched on** in the app.
2. **At least one active eligible subscription** on the card — for example, a recurring payment such as a streaming service charged to the card.

Miss either condition and you earned nothing, however much you spent. The cap was **£15/€15 per month**, which meant spending beyond €1,000 a month earned no additional reward. The card itself is issued by **Paynetics** and is available in the UK and most of the EEA, with a daily spending limit of **£9,000**. ([Sources: Investing in the Web](https://investingintheweb.com/brokers/trading-212-cashback/); [Be Clever With Your Cash](https://becleverwithyourcash.com/trading-212-card-review/))

## What Stays After 3 December

The card does not disappear — only the cashback does. Two features remain relevant:

- **Low FX fee:** the 0.15% foreign-exchange fee stays in place after the cashback ends, which still makes the card cheap for spending in other currencies compared with traditional bank cards. ([Source: Be Clever With Your Cash](https://becleverwithyourcash.com/trading-212-card-review/))
- **Interest on cash:** the interest Trading 212 pays on uninvested cash in the account is a separate feature from card cashback and is not part of this change.

So after 3 December the Trading 212 card becomes a low-cost travel and spending card attached to an investment account — useful, but no longer a reason to route all your everyday spending through it. If cashback was why you used it, you need a new home for that spending. ## The Alternatives, Compared Honestly

The table below is built from a current comparison of European cashback cards. ([Source: Bleap, best cashback cards in Europe](https://www.bleap.finance/blog/best-cashback-cards-europe)) Read the "catch" column before the percentage column — that is where the real price sits.

| Card | Headline cashback | Monthly fee | FX fee | The catch |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bleap** | Up to 20% in USDC | €0 | 0% | "Up to" is a maximum, not a typical rate; paid in a crypto stablecoin, not euros |
| **Revolut Metal** | RevPoints (cashback retired) | ~€15.99+/mo | Plan-dependent | No cash cashback anymore — points, and you pay a premium plan fee |
| **Crypto.com Visa** | Up to 5% in CRO | Tier-dependent | Tier-dependent | Top rates require staking CRO; reward is a volatile token |
| **Vivid Prime** | 1% flat, up to 4% in categories | €9.90/mo | Plan-dependent | The €9.90 fee eats the 1% on moderate spending |
| **Amex (EU)** | Points-based | Card-dependent | Card-dependent | Points, not cash; acceptance in Europe is patchier than Visa/Mastercard |
| **Nexo Card** | Up to 2% | Tier-dependent | Tier-dependent | Paid in crypto; higher tiers depend on your Nexo balance |

Three warnings apply to almost every row:

- **"Up to" means maximum, not typical.** A card advertising "up to 20%" or "up to 5%" is quoting its best tier under its best conditions — usually a specific spending category, a monthly cap, or a balance/staking requirement. Your everyday grocery spend will almost certainly earn a lower rate. Never budget with the headline number.
- **Paid in crypto is not paid in euros.** Cashback in USDC, CRO or another token exposes you to conversion spreads and, for non-stablecoins, price swings between earning and spending the reward. A 5% reward in a token that falls 10% before you convert it is not 5%.
- **Fees cancel cashback.** A €9.90 or €15.99 monthly plan fee needs €990–€1,599 of spending at 1% just to break even. Paid plans only make sense if you use their other benefits.

One more option deserves a mention because it works like Trading 212's did: **Trade Republic's Saveback**, which invests a percentage of card spending into an ETF savings plan instead of paying cash. If you liked the "spending that invests itself" model, that is the closest like-for-like replacement — see our [Trade Republic review](/en/blog/trade-republic-review-2026) for the conditions before deciding.

## Worked Example: €800 a Month of Spending

Assumptions: €800/month of eligible everyday spending, all rates as sourced above, no category bonuses unless stated.

- **Trading 212 (until 3 Dec):** 1.5% of €800 = **€12/month** (€144/year), invested into a Pie. From 3 December: **€0**.
- **Vivid Prime at 1% flat:** €8/month, minus the €9.90 plan fee = **−€1.90/month** unless you use the category boosts (up to 4%) enough to cover the fee. At €800/month of plain spending, the paid plan loses money.
- **Nexo Card at its 2% maximum:** €16/month — but that is the top tier, paid in crypto, and depends on your balance with Nexo. Treat it as a ceiling, not an expectation.
- **Crypto.com Visa at its 5% maximum:** €40/month in CRO — again a top-tier, staking-dependent ceiling, and the token can move in value before you convert it.
- **Bleap at its 20% maximum:** €160/month in USDC on paper. This is precisely the kind of marketing maximum you should not plan around: the comparison does not present 20% as the standard rate for all spending, and earnings depend on the programme's tiers and caps. The genuinely attractive parts of the offer are the €0 fee and 0% FX — the cashback rate you will actually get needs checking in the app's terms before you rely on it.
- **Revolut Metal:** no cash cashback to compute. You earn RevPoints and pay ~€15.99+/month for the plan; whether that is worth it depends on lounge access, insurance and other Metal benefits, not on cashback.

The honest summary: at €800/month, **nothing on the market cleanly replaces €12/month of unconditional-ish cashback**. The realistic replacements either pay less, charge a fee that offsets the reward, or pay in crypto with conditions attached.

## Verdict by Spender Type

- **Everyday EU spender who wants euros back:** there is no like-for-like 1.5% replacement. Vivid Prime's 1% flat rate only beats zero if your spending (or category boosts) comfortably clears the €9.90 fee — roughly €1,000+/month of eligible spend. Below that, a free card with no cashback and no fee can genuinely be the better deal.
- **Traveller:** keep the Trading 212 card for its 0.15% FX fee, or look at Bleap's 0% FX with no monthly fee. Cashback matters less than the exchange rate on a holiday budget.
- **Crypto-comfortable spender:** Bleap (USDC, €0 fee), Nexo (up to 2%) and Crypto.com Visa (up to 5% CRO) are the highest headline rates — provided you accept token-denominated rewards, possible staking/balance tiers, and conversion costs. Read the tier table, not the banner.
- **"Invest my cashback" fan:** Trade Republic's Saveback is the closest spiritual successor to Trading 212's Pie model. Check the savings-plan conditions in our [review](/en/blog/trade-republic-review-2026) first.

## What to Do This Week

1. **Use the final days.** Cashback runs until 3 December. If you have a large planned purchase, bringing it forward a few days earns 1.5% on it (subject to the €15 monthly cap — there is no benefit in spending past €1,000 this month for cashback purposes).
2. **Check your conditions are met.** Confirm "Invest cashback" is on and at least one eligible subscription is active on the card, or the final month's cashback pays nothing.
3. **Move standing payments.** From 3 December, subscriptions and recurring payments on the Trading 212 card earn nothing. Move them to whichever card you choose next so the new card starts earning (and, for cards with activity conditions, qualifies) from day one.
4. **Check exclusions on the new card.** Before switching, verify which transaction types are excluded from cashback (common exclusions: ATM withdrawals, transfers, top-ups, gambling, tax payments) and what the monthly cap is. The cap, not the rate, decides your real earnings.
5. **Do not chase the headline.** If a card's best rate requires staking, a paid plan, or spending in narrow categories, compute your own effective rate at your real monthly spend — as in the worked example above — before signing up.

Trading 212's cashback was a genuinely good deal while it lasted: 1.5% flat with a simple €15 cap. Its disappearance leaves a gap that no European card currently fills on the same terms. The best response is not to grab the biggest "up to" number on a landing page, but to pick the card whose *effective* rate — after fees, caps and conditions — is highest at the amount you actually spend.
