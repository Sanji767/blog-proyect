# Wise Review 2026: Fees, Exchange Rates & Is It Worth It?

Wise (formerly TransferWise) is the benchmark for moving money across borders without the hidden markup traditional banks hide inside the exchange rate. Founded in 2011, it now serves [19 million active customers](https://hodlometer.com/learn/wise-review/) (year to 31 March 2026) — and its pitch has barely changed: you get the real mid-market rate, and the fee is shown as a separate line before you confirm anything. The [Wise profile](/en/bancos/wise) summarises fees, the Belgian IBAN and limits on one page.

This review is based on Wise's own pricing pages for EEA customers, checked on 20 November 2026. Fees vary slightly by country of residence, so treat the figures below as the Netherlands/EEA price list and check [Wise's pricing page](https://wise.com/nl/pricing/) for your own country before signing up.

## The multi-currency account

The core product is a multi-currency account, not a bank account. You can hold and convert more than 40 currencies, and you get local account details in 22 of them — including EUR, USD, GBP, AUD, CAD, NZD and SGD — free of charge. That means a client in the US can pay you into US account details, a client in the UK into UK sort code and account number, and a European client into a euro IBAN, all without them paying international transfer fees.

For euros, Wise gives you account details with a Belgian IBAN and BIC (Wise Europe SA is based in Belgium). That works fine for salary, SEPA direct debits and everyday euro payments — under EU rules, no payer in the eurozone may refuse a euro IBAN from another EU country — but it is not a Dutch IBAN, and Wise is not on the iDEAL issuer list, so you cannot use it for iDEAL payments in the Netherlands.

Receiving money is free for domestic (non-SWIFT) payments in AUD, CAD, EUR, GBP, HUF, NZD, PHP, SGD and USD. SWIFT payments cost a fixed fee per payment: €2.39 for euros, £2.16 for pounds and $6.11 for US dollars. Sending money between Wise contacts in the same currency is free.

## Exchange rates and transfer fees

Wise converts at the mid-market rate — the rate you see on Google — and charges a separate fee that varies by currency pair, [from 0.2%](https://wise.com/nl/pricing/). There is no weekend markup: the rate and the fee are the same on Saturday as on Tuesday, which is a genuine difference from Revolut, which adds a weekend surcharge on its free plan.

The fee is always shown before you confirm, and volume discounts kick in on larger transfers. Independent checks in October 2026 put a EUR to USD conversion at around 0.46%, or about €4.60 per €1,000 — but the exact figure moves by pair and by payment method, so the only number that counts is the one [Wise's calculator](https://wise.com/nl/pricing/) shows you for your transfer.

The honest comparison: Wise is never free for a conversion. If you convert less than €1,000 a month on weekdays, Revolut Standard does that for nothing. But once you pass that allowance, or convert at weekends, or move larger amounts, Wise's transparent percentage usually wins — and its rate never carries a hidden markup.

## The Wise debit card

The Wise debit card is a Visa/Mastercard debit card linked to your balances. It costs [€7 once](https://wise.com/nl/pricing/), with no monthly or annual fee, and a virtual card for Apple Pay or Google Pay is free. A replacement card costs €4 and optional express delivery starts at €10.40.

Spending works the way you would hope: pay in a currency you already hold and there is no Wise fee at all. Pay in a currency you do not hold and Wise converts automatically at the mid-market rate plus the standard conversion fee for that pair. There is no extra card markup on top.

ATM withdrawals are free up to €250 per month per account. Above that, Wise charges 2.69% of the amount over €250. Since 1 May 2026 there is no cap on the number of free withdrawals — only the €250 amount matters. The ATM's own operator can still add a surcharge, which Wise does not control.

## Wise Interest: earn on your balance

Wise Interest lets you earn a return on EUR, GBP and USD balances by investing them in a money market fund — for euros, the BlackRock ICS Euro Government Liquidity Fund, offered through Wise Assets Europe AS. As of 10 November 2026, the variable rate on euros was [2.32%](https://wise.com/ee/interest/), after an annual fee of 0.26% (0.16% to Wise, 0.10% to the fund manager). GBP paid 3.47% and USD 3.66% on the same date.

Three things to understand before using it:

- **It is an investment, not a savings account.** The rate is variable, based on the fund's 7-day performance, and tracks the ECB rate. Growth is not guaranteed and your capital is at risk if governments default or rates go negative.
- **It is not deposit-insured.** Money in Wise Interest sits in a fund, not a bank deposit, so no deposit guarantee scheme covers it.
- **The fee is already deducted from the headline rate.** The 2.32% figure is what you get after the 0.26% annual fee.

At 2.32%, Wise Interest beats most Dutch high-street savings accounts and is competitive with the better flexible savings options in Europe — but it is not the highest return available, and it carries investment risk that a plain savings account does not. Wise also offers a Stocks feature (from 0.59% a year in fees) for investing in funds, which is a separate product with the same capital-at-risk warning.

## Is Wise safe?

This is the section most reviews get wrong, so here it is plainly.

Wise is **not a bank**. In the EU, your account is with Wise Europe SA, an authorised payment institution supervised by the National Bank of Belgium. Wise does not lend your money out, and your funds are [safeguarded](https://wise.com/help/articles/50VrYRVwHcsYeKzvWbjf3n/how-our-eu-entity-wise-europe-sa-safeguards-customer-funds/5xyR2VWzi3.midi): kept separate from Wise's own money, in cash at leading commercial banks (Wise names JPMorgan Chase) and low-risk liquid assets such as money market funds managed by BlackRock and State Street.

Safeguarding is real protection — if Wise failed, your money is ring-fenced and not part of its assets. But it is **not** the same as a deposit guarantee. Wise states this explicitly: its payment services are not subject to the Belgian Deposit Guarantee Scheme, so there is no government-backed payout promise of up to €100,000 the way a licensed EU bank offers. Money invested through the separate Wise Assets service has its own protection under the Estonian Guarantee Fund, up to €20,000 per customer.

Practical conclusion: Wise is a well-regulated, publicly listed company with a solid safeguarding setup, and it is a good place to hold spending money and receive payments. It is not the right place to park your entire emergency fund — keep larger balances in a licensed bank with deposit insurance.

## Wise Business

For freelancers and small businesses, Wise Business adds invoicing, batch payments and accounting integrations for a [€60 one-off setup fee](https://expatnetherlandshub.com/guides/finance/wise-review-expats-netherlands/) (no monthly subscription). The same mid-market rates and fee structure apply. If you invoice clients in several currencies, the local account details alone can be worth it.

## Pros and cons

**Pros:**

- Real mid-market exchange rate with no markup, seven days a week — no weekend surcharge
- Fully transparent fees, shown before you confirm, from 0.2% per conversion
- Free account with no monthly fee; local account details in 22 currencies
- Wise Interest pays a competitive variable return on EUR balances (2.32% as of 10 Nov 2026)
- Funds safeguarded and never lent out; regulated in the EU by the National Bank of Belgium
- Excellent for freelancers receiving payments in several currencies

**Cons:**

- Not a bank: no deposit guarantee on your balance
- Every conversion costs a fee — small weekday conversions are free on Revolut Standard
- Belgian IBAN, not a local one; no iDEAL support in the Netherlands
- ATM free allowance (€250/month) is modest for heavy cash users
- Wise Interest is an investment product — capital at risk, rate moves with the market
- No cashback, no credit products, no branch network

## Verdict

Wise remains the best tool in Europe for one specific job: moving and spending money in more than one currency at the real exchange rate, with the fee stated upfront. For expats, freelancers with foreign clients and frequent travellers, the combination of a free account, local details in 22 currencies, a €7 card and no weekend markup is hard to beat.

It is not a replacement for a bank account. The absence of deposit insurance, the Belgian IBAN and the lack of iDEAL mean most people in the Netherlands will still want a local bank alongside it. Use Wise for what it is brilliant at — currency — and keep your savings in a licensed bank.
