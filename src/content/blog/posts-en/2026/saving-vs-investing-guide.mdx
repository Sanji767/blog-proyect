# Saving vs. Investing: Which Should You Do First?

Understanding when to save and when to invest is the single most important rule in personal finance.

## What is Saving?
Saving means keeping cash in ultra-safe, liquid accounts (like high-yield savings accounts or emergency funds). 

- **Purpose**: Emergency expenses, short-term goals (under 3 years).
- **Risk**: Extremely low risk of nominal capital loss, but high risk of erosion due to inflation.

## What is Investing?
Investing means allocating capital into assets (like index funds, stocks, real estate, or ETFs) that have the potential to outpace inflation and generate compound interest.

- **Purpose**: Long-term wealth generation, retirement (5+ years horizon).
- **Risk**: Market volatility in the short term, but historical growth in the long term.

## The 4-Step Rule for Beginners
1. **Pay off high-interest debt** (anything above 6-7% APR).
2. **Build an emergency fund** equal to 3 to 6 months of expenses.
3. **Invest in low-cost global index funds** (e.g. MSCI World or S&P 500).
4. **Automate monthly contributions**.
