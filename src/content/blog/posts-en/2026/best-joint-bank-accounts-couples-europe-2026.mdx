# Best Joint Bank Accounts for Couples in Europe (2026)

Sharing money with a partner used to mean a trip to a branch and a pile of paperwork. In 2026, the best joint accounts in Europe open in minutes from an app — but not every "shared" feature is a real joint account. Some products give both partners full legal ownership of the money; others just let one person share access to their own account. That distinction matters most exactly when things go wrong.

## Joint account vs. shared pot: the distinction that matters

A true joint account has two legal owners. Both holders own the money, both can spend it, and both are liable for what happens with it. A shared pot or "space" is different: one person owns the funds and grants the other permission to use them. The second person has access, not ownership — which decides who keeps the money if the relationship ends, and means deposit protection (€100,000 per holder) covers up to €200,000 for a couple on a joint account rather than €100,000 for a single owner. Several well-known "couple" features are pots, not joint accounts.

## N26: a free joint account with a dedicated IBAN

N26 now offers a genuine joint account, which corrects a common piece of outdated advice: older reviews said N26 had no joint accounts, and that is no longer true. The [N26 joint account](https://n26.com/en-eu/crm-joint-account) is an easy-access shared account for two people with its own dedicated IBAN, so you can run shared direct debits for rent and utilities from it and link a virtual or physical N26 card to pay directly from the shared balance. Each account holder has full legal ownership of the shared funds, and both are jointly and severally liable — N26 states that each holder carries 100% liability for the account's activity.

It is a free feature on all plans, including free Standard. Both partners need their own N26 account under the same country's terms, and you can open a maximum of one joint account with one other person. Deposit protection of €100,000 applies to each holder individually.

### N26 Shared Spaces: useful, but not a joint account

Shared Spaces are sub-accounts you can share with up to 10 other N26 users, available only on premium plans (Smart, Go and Metal). They have no dedicated IBAN and you cannot link a card to them, so you cannot pay bills directly from a Shared Space. Critically, only the person who opened the Space legally owns the funds in it: other participants act under a power of attorney. N26's own terms spell out that a Shared Space "is not a traditional joint account where both users involved jointly hold/own the account and the funds within the account", and that the owner can revoke the power of attorney and close the space at any time, with the funds returning to the owner's main account.

## Revolut: free joint account plus joint savings

Revolut's [joint account](https://www.revolut.com/en-PT/open-joint-bank-account/) is free, opens in minutes from the app, and gives both holders full access with no account maintenance fees and no minimum deposit. Each of you gets your own card — matching physical cards or virtual ones — and you can pair them with dedicated Pockets to budget. To open one, both of you must be at least 18, have a personal Revolut account with identity verification completed, live in the same country, belong to the same Revolut entity, and not already hold another Revolut joint account. One rule to know upfront: you both own an equal share of the funds even if one of you deposits significantly more.

Where Revolut pulls ahead is its [Joint Savings account](https://www.revolut.com/en-NL/savings/joint-savings-account/), which pays up to 2.75% p.a. (variable) in the Netherlands, with interest paid daily, no minimum deposit and no withdrawal fees, on balances up to €100,000. You need a joint bank account first to open joint savings. Note that the rate depends on your plans: in the UK, for example, the rate paid is the lower of the two holders' plans, so the headline rate only applies if both of you are on the same tier.

Revolut's older Group Vaults feature still exists as a shared savings pot: multiple people can contribute, but only the administrator can withdraw money. Like N26's Shared Spaces, it is a pot for a shared goal, not a joint account.

## bunq: the most flexible joint account, up to 10 co-owners

bunq's [joint account](https://www.bunq.com/en-es/personal/use-cases/couples) is the most flexible of the three fintech options. A joint account can have up to 10 co-owners, all with equal rights, and you do not need to be married or registered partners to open one. Each co-owner needs their own bunq account first, and each of you gets your own card, which you can link to the joint account for shared spending or to your personal account — and switch between them at any time.

A joint account is included in the Core (€3.99/month), Pro (€9.99/month) and Elite (€18.99/month) plans at no extra cost; on the free plan you can join a joint account someone else opens. The joint account gets the IBAN of whoever creates it, with local IBANs available in seven countries — Belgium, Germany, France, Ireland, Spain, Italy and the Netherlands — and a Dutch IBAN otherwise. That matters if your landlord or employer is picky about IBAN country codes. Joint savings accounts earn up to 3.11% interest, paid weekly, though that is the maximum bonus rate: after the bonus period, balances earn a base rate of 1.61% on the first €10,000 and 1.51% above that. Deposits are covered up to €100,000 per user by the Dutch Deposit Guarantee Scheme.

## Traditional banks: ING and ABN AMRO in the Netherlands

If you want a branch, a local IBAN and a bank your employer and landlord instantly recognise, a traditional bank still does the job. In the Netherlands, ING offers an *en/of* (and/or) joint current account: you add a second account holder to your plan for €1.20 a month on ING Go (€4.00/month), included free on the More, Extra and Max plans, and a free Orange savings account comes with the first account. ABN AMRO offers a joint account with two debit cards for €5.80 a month, but both holders need to be 18 or over, have an official Dutch home address, be liable to tax in the Netherlands and hold a BSN. The trade-off is clear: traditional joint accounts cost a few euros a month and require local registration, but they come with the strongest local acceptance for salary, direct debits and iDEAL.

## What happens if you split up?

- **Revolut:** either holder can close the joint account directly in the app, but first the balance must be brought to zero. Either holder can withdraw money without the other's consent, so if trust breaks down, move your share to your personal account first. Closing deactivates linked cards and both co-holders receive their joint statements by email.
- **N26:** both holders have full legal ownership and joint liability, so neither can unilaterally claim the whole balance as "theirs" — but either can spend it. Agree in advance how you will split the balance and close the account together, and move shared direct debits before closing.
- **bunq:** co-owners cannot be removed or changed once assigned. If you split up, your practical option is to empty the joint account by agreement and stop using it — you cannot simply remove an ex-partner from it. For that reason, bunq's joint account suits established couples better than new relationships.
- **Shared pots (N26 Shared Spaces, Revolut Group Vaults):** the owner or administrator controls the money. With N26, the owner can revoke access and close the space at any time, and the funds return to the owner. If you contributed to a partner's pot, you have no legal claim to get your share back — another reason to use a real joint account for serious shared money.

The simplest protection, whatever bank you choose: keep your salary and personal savings in your own account, fund the joint account only with what you both agree to share, and never let the joint balance grow larger than you could afford to argue about.

## Which joint account should you choose?

- **Best free option:** N26 or Revolut — both offer a genuine joint account with a dedicated IBAN and cards for both holders at no cost. Pick N26 for a German bank and German IBAN; pick Revolut for joint savings at up to 2.75% and multi-currency spending.
- **Best for larger households:** bunq — up to 10 co-owners with equal rights and local IBANs in seven countries, from €3.99 a month. Just remember co-owners cannot be removed.
- **Best for local acceptance:** a traditional bank such as ING (from €4.00/month plus €1.20 for the second holder) if a local IBAN and branch access matter more than a slick app.
- **Best for a shared goal, not shared bills:** N26 Shared Spaces or Revolut Group Vaults — pots for holidays and gifts, where one person owning the funds is acceptable.

Rates and fees change, so check the provider's own price list before you open an account. But the structure does not change: choose a real joint account for shared living costs, and keep pots for what they are — a way to save together, not to bank together.
