# Trade Republic Review 2026: 4% Interest, €1 Trades and the Honest Downsides

Trade Republic has quietly become one of the most useful apps in European personal finance: a broker that pays real interest on your idle cash, charges €1 per trade, and lets you automate ETF investing from €1 a month. With over 10 million customers in 18 European countries, the Berlin-based firm is now a fully licensed bank, regulated by Germany's BaFin and supervised by the Deutsche Bundesbank — it received its full banking licence from the ECB in December 2023.

The headline this November is the rate on cash: 2.50% a year (2.53% AER) on your whole uninvested balance as standard, and 4.00% a year (4.07% AER) for new clients on up to €20,000 until 1 March 2027 in several countries, including Spain, Italy, the Netherlands, Portugal, Ireland, Belgium, Austria, Finland and Slovenia. That makes it worth a serious look both as a savings account and as a first broker — but the rate is variable, the promo ends, and the investing side has quirks. Here is the full picture. The [Trade Republic profile](/en/bancos/trade-republic) summarises rates, fees, IBAN and requirements on one page.

## Interest on cash: the strongest reason to open an account

Trade Republic pays interest on uninvested cash, accrued daily and paid monthly. The standard rate rose from 2.25% to 2.50% on 16 September 2026, the day the ECB raised its deposit facility rate to 2.50% — and the ECB has held that rate since 29 October 2026. In other words, the rate tracks the ECB: if the central bank cuts, Trade Republic's rate will fall; if it hikes again in December, as chief economist Philip Lane has suggested would be prudent, the rate could rise.

The new-client offer is genuinely generous: 4.00% a year (4.07% AER) on balances up to €20,000 until 1 March 2027. On a full €20,000 that is roughly €800 of interest over a year at the promo rate, against about €500 at the standard 2.50%. After the promo ends, or above €20,000, the standard 2.50% applies. Note that in Germany the new-client offer is 2.50%, not 4.00% — the higher rate applies in the other listed markets.

Trade Republic wins on the combination of a high standard rate, no monthly fee, no balance cap on the standard rate, and no paid plan required. The only providers beating its standard 2.50% are Scalable Capital at 2.60% and bunq's conditional 3.01% bonus rate — and both come with their own conditions.

One important nuance: Trade Republic holds client cash with partner banks and, for amounts beyond that arrangement, in money market funds. Cash held with partner banks is covered by the German deposit guarantee up to €100,000 per customer. Money held in a money market fund is an investment, not a deposit, so it is not covered by that guarantee. In practice this matters most for large balances: keep your emergency fund under the €100,000 guarantee limit, and read our guide to [money market funds vs savings accounts](/en/blog/money-market-funds-vs-savings-accounts-2026) before parking a large sum anywhere.

## Investing: €1 trades and free savings plans

Trade Republic is first and foremost a broker, and its pricing is simple: €1 per transaction for stocks, ETFs, derivatives, bonds and crypto at the default execution venue (Best Price), or €2 if you choose the execution venue yourself (Direct Price). There are no account, custody or inactivity fees.

The real value is in the savings plans. You can automate investments in over 4,900 stocks, ETFs, bonds and crypto assets from €1, with no per-trade fee at all. Plans execute on the 2nd, 9th, 16th or 23rd of the month, weekly or monthly, funded by direct debit or from your account balance. For a monthly ETF investor, that means genuinely free investing: €0 per purchase, every month, indefinitely.

The range covers more than 2,000 ETFs, plus stocks, bonds, derivatives and real crypto. In July 2026, following the EU-wide ban on payment for order flow (PFOF), Trade Republic overhauled how it executes orders — worth knowing, because the old model of "free" trading subsidised by order-flow payments is gone across the EU, and the €1 flat fee is what replaced it.

Compared with the other low-cost brokers we track in our [best low-cost brokers in Europe](/en/blog/best-low-cost-stock-brokers-europe-2026) guide, Trade Republic is cheapest for the classic use case — a monthly ETF savings plan — and competitive for one-off trades. Scalable Capital can be cheaper for large one-off ETF purchases (free above €250 on its PRIME+ plan), but it charges for the privilege, while Trade Republic has no monthly plan at all.

## The debit card and Saveback

Trade Republic also issues a debit card (virtual or physical) linked to your account balance, with no separate card subscription fee. Two features stand out:

- **Saveback:** you earn 1% of your card spending back — capped at €15 a month — provided you invest at least €50 a month through a savings plan. The reward goes into your investment plan, not as cashback to spend.
- **Round-ups:** spare change from purchases can be rounded up and invested automatically.

Free ATM withdrawals start from €100. It is a competent everyday card, though it is not a travel card: unlike Revolut, there is no free currency exchange allowance up to €1,000 a month, so for spending outside the eurozone a dedicated [no-FX-fee travel card](/en/blog/best-no-fx-fee-travel-cards-2026) remains the better tool.

## Safety: a real bank, with real limits

Trade Republic Bank GmbH holds a full German banking licence and is regulated by BaFin and supervised by the Deutsche Bundesbank. That puts it in a different category from e-money fintechs: it is a bank, and cash held with its partner banks is covered by the German deposit guarantee scheme up to €100,000 per customer.

That said, three limits matter:

1. **The interest rate is variable.** It tracks the ECB deposit rate. A cut in Frankfurt flows through to your balance, and the 4.00% promo is time-limited to 1 March 2027.
2. **Investments are not deposits.** Stocks, ETFs and crypto carry market risk, and crypto in particular can fall sharply. The €100,000 guarantee covers cash, not your portfolio.
3. **There are no branches.** Everything runs through the app. Support is in-app, which suits most users but is a real difference from a traditional bank if you ever need to walk into an office.

## Who should use it — and who should not

**It is a strong fit if you:**

- Want your emergency fund to earn a real return without locking it away. At 2.50% on the whole balance with instant access, it beats nearly every traditional bank savings account. See how it stacks up in our [best high-yield savings accounts in Europe](/en/blog/best-high-yield-savings-accounts-europe-2026).
- Invest monthly in ETFs and want that to cost €0. The free savings plans are the cheapest way to build a portfolio in Europe.
- Want one app for cash, investing and everyday spending, without paying a monthly subscription.

**Look elsewhere if you:**

- Need a multi-currency account or a travel card — [Revolut](/en/blog/revolut-review-2026) is better for that.
- Want a fixed rate you can rely on for a year or two — a fixed-term deposit locks the rate, while Trade Republic's moves with the ECB.
- Hold well over €100,000 in cash and want all of it guaranteed — you would need to spread it across banks, or accept money market fund risk for the excess.

## Verdict

Trade Republic is the best all-rounder in European fintech right now for one specific person: someone who wants their cash to earn interest, their ETF investing to be free, and their banking to cost nothing per month. The 4.00% new-client rate until March 2027 is the best easy rate available in Spain, Italy, the Netherlands and the other listed markets, and the 2.50% standard rate that follows it is still among the highest unconditional rates in Europe.

The honest caveats are the ones that apply to every broker offering interest on cash: the rate follows the ECB, the promo ends, and money swept into funds is not a guaranteed deposit. None of that is a reason to avoid it — it is a reason to use it for what it is good at, keep balances within the €100,000 guarantee, and check the rate again when the ECB meets on 17 December.
