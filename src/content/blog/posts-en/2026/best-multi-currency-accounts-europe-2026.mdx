# Best Multi-Currency Accounts in Europe 2026

A multi-currency account lets you hold, receive and spend money in several currencies without converting everything through your euro account first. If you get paid in dollars, spend in pounds, or travel outside the eurozone, that is not a nice extra — it is where your money quietly leaks away. A traditional bank typically hides a [3% markup](https://bestmulticurrencyaccount.com/) in the exchange rate, while the best multi-currency accounts charge well under 1% or nothing at all on smaller amounts.

This guide compares the five strongest options for European residents in 2026: Wise, Revolut, bunq, N26 and FinecoBank. Figures were checked against provider pricing pages in October–November 2026; prices vary slightly by country, so confirm the live figure in the app before a large conversion.

## Wise: the benchmark for holding and converting currencies

Wise is the purest multi-currency account on this list. You can hold more than 40 currencies, and you get [local account details to receive money in 22 currencies](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut), including USD, GBP, EUR, AUD and SGD — so a US client can pay you by domestic transfer as if you had a US bank account. There is no monthly fee at all.

Every conversion uses the mid-market rate — the rate you see on Google — plus a fee shown upfront: [from 0.2%, and 0.46% for EUR to USD](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut) in October 2026 (€4.58 per €1,000). There is no weekend markup. The physical card costs [€7 one-off](https://topewallets.com/reviews/wise), virtual cards are free, and ATM withdrawals are [free up to €250 a month, then 2.69%](https://topewallets.com/reviews/wise).

One thing to understand: Wise is not a bank. In the EU it operates as Wise Europe SA, a payment institution supervised by the National Bank of Belgium, and balances are safeguarded rather than covered by a deposit guarantee. [Wise Interest pays 2.25% on EUR](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut) (after 0.26% annual fees, as of 23 September 2026) through a money market fund — but capital is at risk and investor protection covers only up to €20,000.

**Best for:** freelancers and remote workers who get paid in several currencies, and anyone converting larger amounts or spending on weekends.

## Revolut: the best free everyday account with 38 currencies

Revolut is a full bank in the EU — Revolut Bank UAB, licensed by the European Central Bank and supervised by the Bank of Lithuania — so deposits are [protected up to €100,000](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut). The free Standard plan gives you a European IBAN and lets you [hold and exchange 38 currencies](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut), with local account details for some currencies and SWIFT details for the rest.

On the free plan, weekday exchanges are [free up to €1,000 per month](https://www.revolut.com/en-NL/legal/standard-fees/), with a 1% fair usage fee above that; on weekends (Friday 5pm to Sunday 6pm, New York time) a [1% fee applies](https://help.revolut.com/en-NL/help/card-payments-withdrawals/getting-started-with-card-payments/can-i-pay-in-a-specific-currency). Crypto and commodity exchanges count towards the same €1,000 limit. Paid plans raise the limits: Plus (€3.99/month in the Netherlands) allows €3,000 with a 0.5% fee above it, while Premium (€10.99), Metal (€19.99) and Ultra (€65) have no exchange limit and no weekend fee. Prices vary by country.

ATM withdrawals on Standard are free up to [5 withdrawals or €200 per rolling month, whichever comes first](https://www.revolut.com/en-NL/legal/standard-fees/), then 2% per withdrawal with a €1 minimum. Paid plans raise the free limit to €400 (Premium), €800 (Metal) or €2,000 (Ultra). The first card is free, with a €7.99 delivery fee on Standard. Savings on the free plan pay [2.50% up to €2,500, then 1.25%](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut), in a real bank savings account covered by the deposit guarantee.

**Best for:** everyday banking in euros with occasional foreign-currency spending under €1,000 a month on weekdays.

## bunq: local IBANs in seven countries and 20 currencies

bunq is a Dutch bank with a full banking licence, supervised by De Nederlandsche Bank, so deposits are protected up to €100,000. Its standout feature is choice of IBAN: you can [get an IBAN from the Netherlands, Germany, France, Spain, Belgium, Italy or Ireland](https://www.bunq.com/personal/features/international-bank-accounts-and-foreign-currencies), which removes friction with landlords and employers who insist on a local account number. You can also hold money in [20 currencies](https://www.bunq.com/personal/features/international-bank-accounts-and-foreign-currencies), including USD, GBP and CHF, and send money in 47 currencies.

Plans run from Free (€0) to Core (€3.99), Pro (€9.99) and Elite (€18.99) per month. Foreign-currency card payments cost the [Mastercard rate plus 0.5%](https://www.bankverse.ch/bunq-review/) on every plan — despite the "ZeroFX" name, bunq's own price sheet describes a 0.5% charge on top of the Mastercard rate. On Free, that rate only covers €1,000 of spending a year, then 1.5% applies. In-app conversion costs the [Currencycloud rate plus a 0.5% network fee](https://help.bunq.com/en-fr/articles/what-are-the-foreign-currencies-accounts-fees), with an extra 0.15%–0.5% at weekends.

Cash is the weak spot: Free charges [€2.99 per ATM withdrawal](https://www.bankverse.ch/bunq-review/), Core €0.99 for the first five then €2.99, and only Pro and Elite include six free withdrawals a month. Receiving SWIFT payments costs €5 each. Pro also unlocks 25 bank accounts, each with its own IBAN.

**Best for:** expats and remote workers who need a local IBAN in a specific EU country and heavy budgeting with sub-accounts.

## N26: a euro account that spends abroad without markups

N26 is a fully licensed German bank, and its Standard plan is free with a German IBAN. Strictly speaking it is not a multi-currency account — you hold euros only — but it earns a place here because card payments are [free in any currency with no foreign-currency surcharge](https://banklist.co/n26): you pay at the Mastercard rate, with no exchange limit and no weekend markup. For someone who earns in euros and spends abroad, that is often all that is needed.

The catch is cash. Free euro ATM withdrawals are limited to [2 a month on Standard, 3 on Smart (€4.90), 5 on Go (€9.90) and 8 on Metal (€16.90)](https://banklist.co/n26), then €2 each. Withdrawals in other currencies cost [1.7% on Standard and Smart](https://banklist.co/n26), and are free on Go and Metal. A physical card costs €10 one-off on Standard. Spaces sub-accounts (up to 10, from Smart up) share one IBAN, unlike bunq's separate IBANs. Savings pay [0.30% a year on Standard and Smart, 0.50% on Go and 1.50% on Metal](https://n26.com/en-eu/plans).

**Best for:** euro earners who mostly pay by card abroad and rarely need foreign cash.

## FinecoBank: the traditional-bank option for investors

If you want a multi-currency account inside a traditional bank, FinecoBank is the most complete European option. Its Multicurrency service lets you [hold, convert, receive, send and spend in more than 20 currencies](https://uk.finecobank.com/banking/multicurrency/), with currency exchange at 0 commission — just a markup — and conversion available [21 hours a day, 7 days a week](https://uk.finecobank.com/banking/multicurrency/). There are no monthly management fees, and deposits are covered by the [Italian deposit protection scheme up to €100,000](https://sendmoneycompare.com/guides/multi-currency-account-wars-2026).

The FX spread on EUR/USD is about [0.3%](https://brokaro.io/en/brokers/fineco), competitive for a bank but wider than Wise's fee on the same route. The appeal is combining banking with investing: trade US stocks in dollars without converting every time. The drawbacks are practical: the service is [primarily in Italian, and accounts for non-residents are finalised at a Fineco Center, not online](https://brokaro.io/en/brokers/fineco).

**Best for:** investors who want multi-currency banking and stock trading in one traditional bank account.

## What €1,500 of dollar spending actually costs

What does €1,500 of dollar spending in one month cost?

- **Revolut Standard, all on weekdays:** the first €1,000 is free and 1% applies to the remaining €500, so **€5**.
- **Revolut Standard, all on a weekend:** 1% weekend fee on €1,500 (€15) plus 1% fair usage on €500 (€5), so **€20**.
- **Wise:** the conversion fee for EUR to USD is 0.46%, so about **€6.87**, any day of the week.

These figures come from a [side-by-side check of both providers in October 2026](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut). The lesson: Revolut's free plan wins on small weekday spending, Wise wins on weekends and larger amounts. A simple trick on Revolut Standard is to exchange the currency you need in the app on a Friday morning, before the weekend fee starts.

## How to choose

- **You get paid in USD, GBP or other currencies:** Wise, for local account details in 22 currencies and the mid-market rate on every conversion.
- **You spend under €1,000 a month abroad, mostly on weekdays:** Revolut Standard — free, and a real bank with deposit protection.
- **You need a local IBAN in France, Germany, Spain or another EU country:** bunq Pro, the only account here that offers it.
- **You earn in euros and pay by card when travelling:** N26 Standard — no markup on card payments in any currency.
- **You invest in US stocks and want one traditional bank:** FinecoBank Multicurrency.

Many people end up with two accounts: Revolut or N26 for everyday euros, plus Wise for foreign income and large conversions — €0 a month beyond Wise's one-off €7 card.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Wise a bank?** No. In the EU, Wise Europe SA is a payment institution supervised by the National Bank of Belgium. Balances are safeguarded but not covered by a deposit guarantee, unlike Revolut, N26, bunq and FinecoBank, which all offer €100,000 protection.

**Which multi-currency account is cheapest overall?** For small weekday conversions under €1,000 a month, Revolut Standard is free. For larger amounts, weekend spending and receiving foreign income, Wise is usually cheaper and more predictable.
