# Best Free Business Bank Accounts for Freelancers & SMEs in Europe (2026 Guide)

Separating your personal finances from professional income is a fundamental rule for any freelancer or sole trader in Europe. Co-mingling business and personal funds complicates monthly bookkeeping, delays tax deductions, and causes accounting headaches.

In this guide, we review the **best digital business bank accounts in Europe for 2026**, comparing monthly fees, invoicing capabilities, POS card terminal integrations, and tax accounting features.

One honest note upfront: the free landscape has changed since this guide was first written. Qonto and bunq have both launched genuinely free business plans, so there are now **four** free options for freelancers — Qonto Starter, bunq Free Business, SumUp Business and N26 Business. Only Revolut Business has no free plan in Europe, starting at €10 a month. We keep it in this guide because it is the strongest option for multi-currency business banking.

## In-Depth Review of Top 5 Business Accounts

### 1. Qonto — All-in-One Financial Management
Qonto is Europe's leading financial management platform for freelancers, startups, and SMEs.

- **Automated VAT Receipt Scanning:** Snap a photo of any receipt from your phone; Qonto's AI automatically extracts VAT amounts and matches it to your transaction.

The big change in 2026 is Qonto's **free Starter plan**: €0 a month forever, with one account, one virtual card and 5 free transfers a month — enough for a freelancer starting out. Paid plans start at €7 a month (Basic, billed annually), with Smart at €15 and Premium at €25. ([Source: Qonto pricing](https://qonto.com/en-nl/pricing).) Qonto now serves more than 750,000 companies, and its plans connect to over 2,000 external tools — accounting software, e-commerce platforms and invoicing apps.

One important caveat: Qonto is a payment institution supervised by the Banque de France, **not a bank**. Customer funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts, but they are **not covered by a deposit guarantee scheme**. For a working account holding a few thousand euros, that is a manageable risk; for large balances, pair Qonto with a licensed bank. ([Source: Investing in the Web](https://investingintheweb.com/brokers/best-business-bank-accounts-europe/), fees checked October 2026.)

### 2. SumUp Business — Best for In-Person Card Payments
SumUp is ideal for freelancers with physical stores, consulting practices, or pop-up shops. Instant payout facilities deposit client payments directly into your account with no hidden fees.

SumUp's model is the reverse of the others: the account is free and the money is made on card transactions (1.39%–1.95% per transaction depending on country). If you take most payments in person — a market stall, a salon, a café, a craft fair — SumUp's card reader plus free account is the cheapest complete setup in Europe. The account itself is basic: no invoicing suite, no multi-currency, no sub-accounts. Pair it with a proper invoicing tool if your bookkeeping needs more than sales tracking.

### 3. N26 Business — 100% Free Account with 0.1% Cashback
N26 Business provides a zero-fee account for sole proprietors including **0.1% cashback** on all business debit card spending.

N26 is a German bank, so eligible deposits are protected up to €100,000 under the German deposit guarantee scheme — the strongest protection of any free option here. The Standard plan is genuinely free; Smart (€4.90/month), Go (€9.90) and Metal (€16.90) add more Spaces, higher limits and travel perks. The limitation is scope: N26 Business is designed for freelancers and the self-employed only. The account is opened in your own legal name, a company name cannot appear on it, and it is not an option for a limited company. ([Source: Investing in the Web](https://investingintheweb.com/brokers/best-business-bank-accounts-europe/).)

### 4. bunq Business — Best Free Plan for Multiple IBANs
bunq is a Dutch bank (licensed by De Nederlandsche Bank) with a free business plan and paid plans from €7.99 a month, and it is the only free option that gives you multiple IBANs from day one.

- **Free plan:** €0 a month, 3 bank accounts each with its own Dutch IBAN, 1 digital card — for freelancers (sole proprietors) in 9 EU countries (Netherlands, Belgium, France, Germany, Austria, Spain, Italy, Ireland, Portugal).
- **Core (€7.99/month):** 5 accounts. **Pro (€13.99/month):** 25 accounts, up to 25 virtual and 3 physical cards, company cards for employees, Tap to Pay and accounting integrations. **Elite (€23.99/month):** 25 accounts with higher limits.
- **Deposit protection:** eligible deposits are protected up to €100,000 by the Dutch deposit guarantee scheme.

The multiple-IBAN structure is bunq's real advantage for freelancers: one IBAN for income, one for tax savings, one for expenses — the separation your accountant will thank you for. The catch is eligibility: your company must be registered in one of the 9 supported countries, and every director and ultimate beneficial owner must live in the EEA. ([Sources: bunq help centre](https://help.bunq.com/articles/what-plans-are-available); [Investing in the Web](https://investingintheweb.com/brokers/best-business-bank-accounts-europe/).)

### 5. Revolut Business — Best for Multi-Currency and Scale
Revolut Business is the most capable account here — and the only one without a free plan. Basic costs **€10 a month** and includes 10 free local transfers and €1,000 of free currency exchange at the interbank rate; Grow (€35/month) raises that to 100 local transfers and €15,000 of exchange; Scale (€125/month) to 1,000 transfers and €60,000 of exchange. ([Source: Revolut Business pricing](https://www.revolut.com/en-NL/business/business-account-plans/).)

What you get for the money: multi-currency accounts in 30+ currencies, local IBANs for business accounts in France, Ireland, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Romania and Spain, team cards with spending controls, payment acceptance (online and in person), and savings at around 1–1.25% p.a. (variable, depending on country and plan). In the EEA the account is provided by Revolut Bank UAB, licensed by the ECB and the Bank of Lithuania, so eligible deposits are protected up to €100,000. It accepts companies registered in the EEA, Switzerland, the UK, the US, Australia, Singapore and India — the widest eligibility of any account in this guide. The downsides: no free plan, weekend exchanges cost 1%, and ATM withdrawals cost 2%.

## Bank vs. Payment Institution: Why It Matters

Two of the five accounts above are not banks. Qonto and SumUp are payment institutions: your money is safeguarded in segregated accounts at partner banks, which protects it if the company fails — but there is no deposit guarantee scheme paying out €100,000 if something goes wrong at the safeguarding bank itself. N26, bunq and Revolut Bank UAB are licensed banks with full DGS coverage up to €100,000 per depositor per bank under [Directive 2014/49/EU](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/LSU/?uri=CELEX:32014L0049).

For a working account with a few thousand euros flowing through it, any of the five is fine. For balances approaching five figures or more, prefer a licensed bank — or split the money.

## Tax and Bookkeeping Basics for Freelancers

A business account is the foundation, not the whole building:

- **Keep business and personal money fully separate.** Every business expense from the business account, every personal expense from the personal one. Mixed accounts are the single most common bookkeeping headache.
- **Set aside tax money as income arrives.** A common rule is to move 25–30% of each payment into a separate sub-account the day it lands. bunq's multiple IBANs and N26's Spaces are built for exactly this.
- **Keep digital receipts.** Qonto's receipt scanning and bunq's accounting integrations exist because paper receipts get lost. Photograph every receipt the day you get it.
- **Mind the cross-border tax rules.** If your business bank is in another EU country — Revolut in Lithuania, Wise in Belgium — your interest and account data are reported to your home tax authority automatically under CRS, and you declare the income where you live. Our [tax guide for foreign bank accounts](/en/blog/tax-guide-foreign-bank-accounts-europe-2026) covers the forms, including Spain's Modelo 720 and Germany's Freistellungsauftrag.

## FAQ

**Do I legally need a business account as a freelancer?**
In most EU countries, no — sole proprietors are not legally required to hold a separate business account. But it is strongly recommended: it simplifies bookkeeping, makes tax deductions easier to evidence, and keeps an audit clean. In some countries (for example, Germany for certain *Gewerbe* registrations), a separate account is effectively expected by the tax office.

**Can I open a business account if I am not yet registered as a freelancer?**
Most providers ask for proof of self-employment or company registration during onboarding. Qonto and Revolut Business both support company formation flows in some countries; N26 Business and bunq Free require existing freelancer status.

**Is the free plan really free, or is there a catch?**
All four free plans are genuinely €0 a month with no minimum balance. The catch, where there is one, is limits: Qonto Starter allows 5 transfers a month, bunq Free gives 3 accounts and 1 digital card, and N26 Standard has lower ATM and spending limits than its paid plans. If you outgrow the limits, the paid plans start at €7–€10 a month.
