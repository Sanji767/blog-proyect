# Best Cashback Credit Cards in Europe (2026): Earn on Every Purchase

Maximizing credit card rewards is a great way to earn cash back and travel perks on your everyday spending. But Europe is not the United States: EU law caps interchange fees at **0.2% for consumer debit cards and 0.3% for consumer credit cards** (Regulation (EU) 2015/751), which is the money that funds cashback. That is why European cashback rates are structurally lower — typically 0.5% to 1% — and why the best "rewards" card for most Europeans is often a **free card with no foreign transaction fees** rather than a high-rate cashback card.

This guide compares the cards that actually pay you back in Europe in 2026: one true cashback card, the best permanently free credit cards, the Amex points cards, and the debit alternatives people often confuse with credit cards.

## The Cards in Detail

### 1. Bank Norwegian Visa — Best true cashback card in Europe

The [Bank Norwegian Visa](https://creditcardnorway.com/bank-norwegian-credit-card/) is the closest thing Europe has to a straightforward cashback credit card. It charges **no annual fee**, offers **up to 45 interest-free days**, and lets you choose between **0.5% cashback** on everyday purchases (redeemable against your card bill, 1 point = 1 NOK/SEK/DKK) or **CashPoints** in Norwegian's reward programme. CashPoints earn 0.5% on ordinary purchases but **3% on LowFare tickets, 4% on LowFare+ and 5% on Flex tickets** with Norwegian. The card also includes travel and cancellation insurance and free ATM withdrawals.

The catch is availability: Bank Norwegian issues the card in **Norway, Sweden, Finland, Denmark and Germany** only, and you need to live in one of those countries. The nominal interest rate is 21.99% (24.4% effective), so carrying a balance wipes out the cashback many times over.

### 2. Advanzia Mastercard Gold — Best permanently free credit card

The [Advanzia Mastercard Gold](https://www.giromatch.com/en/review/mastercard-gold-advanzia) (known in Germany as the Gebührenfrei Mastercard Gold) is the best free credit card in Europe for expats and travellers — but be clear about what it is: **it pays no cashback**. What it offers instead is a **€0 annual fee forever**, **0% foreign transaction fees worldwide**, free ATM withdrawals globally, and free travel insurance (health, trip cancellation and luggage). You do not need a German bank account — any EU/SEPA IBAN works — and Advanzia, a Luxembourg-based digital bank, is more lenient on thin credit files than traditional German banks. Credit limits can grow to around €20,000–€25,000 with good payment history, and purchases are interest-free for up to seven weeks.

The trap: Advanzia runs **no automatic full direct debit**, so unless you actively transfer the full balance each month, only the minimum is taken and the rest accrues interest at around **24.69–24.74% APR**. Cash withdrawals also accrue interest from day one. Used with discipline — pay in full, every month — it costs nothing; used carelessly, it is one of the most expensive cards on this list.

### 3. Hanseatic Bank GenialCard — Best free card with automatic full payment

The GenialCard from Hanseatic Bank is the other permanently free German credit card: **€0 annual fee, 0% foreign transaction fees**, and no account required. Unlike Advanzia, it is a more conventional card in structure and is often recommended alongside it as one of the two genuinely free options in Germany. It pays no cashback and has no travel insurance worth mentioning — its value is simply that it costs nothing to hold and nothing to use abroad.

### 4. TF Bank Mastercard Gold — Best free card for flexible repayment

TF Bank's Mastercard Gold is the third permanently free option: **€0 annual fee, 0% foreign transaction fees** and free ATM withdrawals. Its revolving APR is around **25.09%**, so the same rule applies as with Advanzia: pay in full or don't use it. TF Bank is a Swedish bank issuing across several European markets, and like Advanzia it states no income floor in its terms, which makes it accessible to students and freelancers.

### 5. American Express Gold (Germany) — Best points card for everyday spenders

The [Amex Gold Card in Germany](https://voyagehacks.com/en/credit-cards/american-express/gold/) costs **€20 a month (€240 a year)** since its 2026 relaunch as a metal card, with no free first year — up from €144 a year. It earns **1 Membership Rewards point per euro** on all spending, with no category fine print, and includes up to **around €450 a year in credits**: €20 a month toward SIXT+, 2 × €25 SIXT rent credit, €5 a month FREENOW credit and a €100 Lodenfrey shopping credit. It also includes family travel insurance and a 90-day return policy. The welcome bonus is up to **50,000 Membership Rewards points through a referral** (20,000 after €3,000 spend, plus 30,000 after a further €2,000), or up to €200 in statement credit when applying direct.

The honest maths: if you use the mobility credits anyway, the card can cost you close to nothing net; if you use none of them, you are paying €240 a year for points and insurance. There is **no lounge access** and no Hilton or Marriott status. Points transfer to Flying Blue, Etihad Guest, Qatar Avios and Emirates — but not to Miles & More. And every German Amex card charges a **2% foreign transaction fee**, which makes it a poor choice for non-euro spending.

### 6. American Express Platinum (Germany) — Best for frequent flyers

The [Amex Platinum](https://voyagehacks.com/en/credit-cards/american-express/platinum-vs-gold/) costs **€60 a month (€720 a year)** and earns the same **1 Membership Rewards point per euro** as the Gold — the extra fee buys perks, not points. Those perks are substantial: access to **over 1,550 airport lounges**, Hilton Honors Gold and Marriott Bonvoy Gold Elite status, comprehensive travel insurance, and **€650 a year in credits** (€200 online travel, €150 dining, €200 SIXT ride, €100 shopping), which nets the card to around €70 a year if every credit is used. The welcome bonus is up to **85,000 points via referral** or up to €340 in statement credit direct.

Two 2026 changes to know: the Platinum **lost Lufthansa lounge access on 1 October 2026** (replaced by six €20 vouchers for Frankfurt and Munich airports), and the Lodenfrey credit now requires a minimum €200 purchase. The 2% foreign transaction fee applies here too.

### 7. American Express Card (Germany) — Best cheap entry to Membership Rewards

If you want Amex points without the Gold or Platinum fee, the basic American Express Card (formerly Green) costs **€60 a year and is free from year two once you spend €9,000 a year**. It earns the same **1 Membership Rewards point per euro**, with no ongoing credits, no lounges and limited travel insurance. For most people who simply want to collect points on everyday spending, this is the rational Amex — the Gold only wins if you use its credits, and the Platinum only if you fly often.

### 8. Revolut Metal — A debit card, not a credit card (and no more cashback)

Revolut Metal appears in many "best cashback cards" lists, but two corrections are needed. First, it is a **debit card**, not a credit card — it spends money you already have. Second, **Revolut discontinued cashback**: Metal no longer pays 0.1% in Europe and 1% outside Europe. It now earns **RevPoints — 1 point per €2 spent** — redeemable for flight miles, hotel discounts or eSIM data. At €1,000 of monthly spend that is roughly 500 points, worth about €3 to €5.

Metal costs **€15.99 a month in Germany** (€155 a year billed annually; France pays €18.99, the Netherlands €19.99, the UK £14.99). What you actually get for the fee is €800 a month in fee-free ATM withdrawals, 3 GB of global eSIM data, travel insurance with rental-car excess cover and winter sports, and lounge passes. It is a reasonable travel bundle — but as a cashback tool, it no longer qualifies. See our [Revolut review](/en/blog/revolut-review-2026) and our guide to the [best cashback debit cards in Europe](/en/blog/best-cashback-debit-cards-europe-2026).

## How to Choose a Rewards Card in Europe

1. **Want actual cash back?** Bank Norwegian is the only true cashback credit card on this list — but only if you live in one of its five countries. Everyone else should look at cashback debit cards instead.
2. **Travel a lot outside the eurozone?** The FX fee decides everything. Advanzia, GenialCard and TF Bank charge 0%; Amex charges 2%, which cancels out its points on foreign spending. Our [best travel cards guide](/en/blog/best-no-fx-fee-travel-cards-2026) compares the no-FX options in detail.
3. **Fly often?** Amex Platinum's lounges and hotel status are worth €720 a year only if you fly double-digit times a year. Otherwise the Gold — or the basic Amex Card — collects the same points for far less.
4. **Just want a free card?** Advanzia, GenialCard and TF Bank all cost €0 forever. Pick Advanzia if you need an EU IBAN rather than a German account, and always pay in full.
5. **Do the net-cost maths.** A €240 or €720 annual fee is only worth it if the credits and perks you will *actually use* exceed the fee. Unused credits are worth €0.

## Downsides and Honest Notes

- **European cashback is small by design.** The EU interchange cap (0.3% on credit cards) means no European issuer can fund US-style 2–5% cashback. Anyone promising that in Europe is usually selling a crypto card where rewards are paid in volatile tokens.
- **Revolving interest destroys rewards.** APRs on the free cards run around 25%. One month of carried balance wipes out a year of 0.5% cashback.
- **Amex charges 2% on foreign currency.** On a €2,000 non-euro trip, that is €40 in fees — more than the points earned are typically worth.
- **Acceptance varies.** Visa and Mastercard are accepted almost everywhere in Europe; Amex acceptance is narrower, especially at small merchants. Keep a Visa or Mastercard as backup.
- **Welcome bonuses have spend thresholds.** Amex's 50,000-point Gold bonus requires €5,000 of spending; never spend extra just to hit a bonus.
- **"Free" cards are not free if you pay interest.** Advanzia's lack of automatic full direct debit is the single most expensive trap on this list — set a calendar reminder to pay the full statement balance every month.

## Tips to Avoid Interest

Never carry a balance from month to month. Set up a **100% direct debit autopay** every month to avoid interest charges — and if your card does not offer automatic full payment (Advanzia does not), transfer the full statement balance yourself as soon as the bill arrives. Use the interest-free period (up to 45 days at Bank Norwegian, up to 7 weeks at Advanzia) as free short-term credit, not as a reason to spend more. And never withdraw cash on a credit card unless you can repay it the same day: cash advances accrue interest immediately, even on cards with free ATM withdrawals.
