# Best Cashback Debit Cards in Europe (2026 Guide)

In 2026, debit cards are no longer just passive payment tools. Leading European neobanks and fintech platforms offer cashback and Saveback programs that automatically refund a percentage of every purchase — into your account, or straight into an ETF investment plan.

But the headline percentages hide the details that actually decide what you earn: monthly caps, activation requirements, and whether the reward arrives as spendable cash or as an investment you cannot touch without selling. One of the best-known programs on this list is also about to disappear entirely. Here is what each card really pays in November 2026.

## Top Cashback Programs Reviewed

### 1. Trade Republic — 1% Saveback Invested in ETFs

Trade Republic's Saveback automatically invests 1% of all eligible debit card payments into a savings plan of your choice — an ETF such as MSCI World, a stock, or another asset. Spending €1,000 a month on groceries, utilities and dining earns €10 a month, invested automatically. The reward is collected during the month and invested on the 2nd of the following month. ([Source: Investing in the Web, Trade Republic Saveback review](https://investingintheweb.com/brokers/trade-republic-saveback-review/))

The conditions matter. You must invest at least €50 a month through savings plans (weekly, twice-monthly or monthly plans count; quarterly plans do not), and Saveback is capped at €15 a month — reached at €1,500 of spending. Payments made directly from your crypto balance earn 2% Saveback instead, up to €30 a month. No Saveback is earned on ATM withdrawals, transfers, top-ups or PayPal transactions.

Trade Republic's card itself is free (virtual card free, physical Classic card €5 one-off), charges no FX fee on card payments, and the cash balance earns 2.50% a year. That combination — interest plus Saveback — makes it the strongest everyday package on this list for people who already invest.

### 2. Trading 212 — 1.5% Cashback, Ending 3 December 2026

Trading 212's 212 Card pays 1.5% cashback on eligible purchases, capped at €15 a month (€1,000 of spending) — but only until 2 December 2026. From 3 December 2026, the cashback rate under the program drops to 0%. Trading 212 has published the termination in its card cashback terms, updated 1 October 2026. ([Source: Trading 212 Help Centre](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/35948436017053-How-does-212-Card-cashback-work))

Even while it lasts, the 1.5% is conditional. Your base rate is 0%: you only earn 1.5% if you switch on "Invest cashback" and have at least one active eligible subscription paid with the card — a recurring payment every 5 days to one month, such as Netflix. And the reward is not cash: it is automatically invested into a Pie of your choice, so its value can go down as well as up.

The card itself remains useful after December — ATM withdrawals are free up to €400 a month (1% after that) and uninvested cash earns interest — but as a cashback card it will be dead. If cashback is your reason for opening a Trading 212 account, do not bother now; pick Trade Republic instead.

### 3. N26 Business — 0.1% to 0.5% Cash Back on Business Spending

N26's business accounts pay cashback on card spending: 0.1% on the free Standard business plan, rising to 0.5% on Metal (€16.90/month). The reward is a direct cash refund, with no monthly cap stated — the honest trade-off being that the rates are low. At 0.1%, you would need to spend €15,000 a month to earn the same €15 that Trade Republic's Saveback pays on €1,500 of spending. ([Source: HeroPay, N26 Business vs Wise Business](https://www.heropay.eu/en/blog/n26-vs-wise/))

N26 also runs a separate promotion for Go and Metal personal customers: 1% cashback for up to 12 months on qualifying transactions made with your N26 Mastercard outside the EEA, Switzerland and the United Kingdom. It is a travel perk rather than an everyday earner, but it stacks with N26's zero FX markup on card payments. ([Source: N26 plans page](https://n26.com/en-eu/plans))

### 4. Revolut — Shops Cashback and Pro Cashback

Revolut has no blanket cashback on everyday personal spending. What it has is two targeted programs. **Revolut Shops** pays up to 3% instant cashback at partner brands — including Amazon, ASOS, Nike and others — as long as you start your shopping journey in the Revolut app and pay with your Revolut card in full. The cashback is applied automatically at checkout, and it stacks with sale prices and promo codes. ([Source: FF News, Revolut Shops launch](https://ffnews.com/news/revolut-launches-a-savvier-way-to-shop-with-up-to-3-cashback))

**Revolut Pro**, the freelance account inside the personal app, pays cashback on business expenses with the Pro debit card: 0.4% on Standard, 0.6% on Premium, 0.8% on Metal and 1% on Ultra. Accepting payments through Pro boosts the rate by up to an extra 1% — up to €120 a month in boosters — once you hit monthly sales targets (€1,200+ for +0.3%, €6,000+ for +0.6%, €12,000+ for +1%). Cashback is paid into your Pro account on the 1st of each month. ([Source: Revolut Help Centre](https://help.revolut.com/en-LT/help/more/revolut-pro/pro-cashback-and-how-does-the-booster-work/))

### 5. Vivid — Up to 4% Cashback on Selected Brands

Vivid offers the highest headline rate on this list: up to 4% cashback on selected brands, with the option to receive the reward as fractional shares ("stock rewards") instead of cash. The catch is in the word "selected" — the rate applies to specific partner brands, not to all spending, and the cap depends on your plan (Standard, Plus at €6.90/month, or Prime at €9.90/month). ([Source: VCard Guide, Vivid Card Review 2026](https://www.vcardguide.com/vivid-card-review))

Vivid's Standard plan costs €4.90 a month, waived if you keep a combined balance of at least €3,000 or spend at least €1,000 a month on the card. It is also an e-money institution rather than a bank, so balances are safeguarded, not deposit-guaranteed. Vivid works best as a second card for spending at its partner brands, not as a main account.

## How to Choose the Right Cashback Card

- **You invest already (or want to start):** Trade Republic. The 1% Saveback plus 2.50% interest on your cash balance is the best combined return, and the €50/month savings plan requirement is money you would be investing anyway.
- **You want cash, not investments:** N26 Business pays real cash back — but at 0.1% to 0.5% it is slow. Revolut Shops pays instant cashback, but only at partner brands. In practice, no European debit card currently pays meaningful uncapped cash back on all spending.
- **You are a freelancer:** Revolut Pro (0.4% to 1%, plus sales boosters) or N26 Business (0.1% to 0.5%) — both pay on business expenses your personal card would not reward.
- **You shop at big online brands:** Revolut Shops (up to 3%) or Vivid (up to 4% on selected brands) for those purchases specifically.

## The Honest Maths: What Cashback Is Really Worth

Every program on this list is capped or restricted, so set expectations accordingly. At Trade Republic's 1% Saveback with its €15 monthly cap, the maximum you can earn is €180 a year — and only if you spend €1,500 a month on the card. At €800 a month of card spending, a realistic figure for many households, you earn €8 a month, or €96 a year. That is real money, but it will not change your finances; it is a bonus on spending you would do anyway, not a reason to spend more.

Two rules keep cashback honest. First, never spend extra to earn a reward worth 1% of the purchase — you lose 99 cents to gain one. Second, watch the conditions: a missed €50 savings plan month at Trade Republic, or a lapsed subscription at Trading 212, drops your rate to zero for that month.

## Downsides and Things to Watch

- **Programs change or end without much notice.** Trading 212's 1.5% cashback — one of the best rates in Europe — ends on 3 December 2026. Check the current terms before you choose a card for its rewards.
- **"Cashback" often is not cash.** Trade Republic's Saveback and Trading 212's cashback are invested automatically. That is good for long-term wealth, but the money is exposed to market risk and is not available to spend.
- **Caps flatten the benefit.** A 1.5% rate sounds 50% better than 1% — but both Trade Republic and Trading 212 cap out at €15 a month, so the real difference is only which spending level reaches the cap (€1,000 vs €1,500).
- **Exclusions are broad.** ATM withdrawals, transfers, top-ups, PayPal and some merchant categories typically earn nothing. Read the exclusions list, not just the headline rate.
