# Best Student Bank Accounts in Europe (2026): Free Accounts for Students

Studying in Europe means paying rent in one country, receiving money from another, and spending in a third on a weekend trip — all on a student budget. The right bank account costs €0 a month, opens from your phone before you arrive, and gives you the IBAN your landlord will accept. The wrong one quietly eats your money in fees and markups.

There is no single best account for every student. A German IBAN matters in Germany, a free currency-exchange allowance matters if you travel, and a student discount matters if you qualify. Here are the six accounts that make sense for students in Europe in 2026 — all free, or free for students.

## N26 Standard — the best free all-rounder

N26 is the simplest answer for most students: a [free account with no monthly fee, no minimum deposit and no minimum income](https://n26.com/en-eu/free-bank-account), from a licensed German bank. You get a German DE IBAN, a free virtual Mastercard, Apple Pay and Google Pay, and free euro payments. You must be [at least 18 years old](https://n26.com/en-eu/plans), and you can open the account in about 10 minutes from your phone — no address registration (*Anmeldung*) and no tax ID needed upfront. Deposits are protected up to €100,000 by the German deposit guarantee scheme.

The catches: foreign-currency card payments carry a ~1.7% markup, so N26 is a poor travel card; free ATM withdrawals are limited to a handful a month; there is no Girokarte; and N26's passport rules exclude some nationalities (Vietnamese, Filipino and Pakistani passports are documented as not accepted). Interest on the free plan is minimal at 0.30%.

**Best for:** any student over 18 who wants one free account with a German IBAN.

## bunq — the best deal if you qualify as a student

bunq is the only account here with a genuine student discount: university students aged 25 and under in Belgium, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, the Netherlands, Portugal and Spain get [bunq Pro — normally €9.99 a month — for free](https://help.bunq.com/articles/free-for-students), or Elite (normally €18.99) for €9, by uploading proof of enrollment. The discount starts on the first day of the next month and ends at graduation or 26, when Pro costs €9.99 — downgrade to the free tier in time.

bunq is also the most accessible first account for non-EU students: it accepts more passports than N26 — including India, China, Vietnam, Indonesia and Brazil — works before address registration, and gives you a German DE IBAN when you sign up in Germany. Savings earn up to 3.01% with the bonus rate, and deposits are covered by the Dutch deposit guarantee up to €100,000.

**Best for:** students ≤25 in the eight eligible countries, and non-EU students whose passport N26 rejects.

## Revolut Standard — the best for travel and for under-18s

Revolut is the strongest second account for students who travel. The Standard plan is free and gives you [€1,000 a month of fee-free currency exchange, then a 1% fee](https://irishtaxmate.ie/blog/how-to-open-revolut-account-ireland) (1% on weekend exchanges too), a free virtual card, and free ATM withdrawals up to 5 withdrawals or €200 a month — after that, a 2% fee applies. A physical card costs €7.99 for delivery. Deposits are covered up to €100,000 by Lithuania's deposit insurance scheme.

Two things to know. First, the IBAN is usually Lithuanian (LT) — fine for spending, but some landlords still refuse non-local IBANs for rent (see below). Second, Revolut is the most flexible on age: the full account needs 18+, but 16- and 17-year-olds can open an account in Ireland and the UK with some products switched off, and [Revolut Kids & Teens](https://www.finder.com/kids-banking/revolut-junior-card) covers ages 6–17 through a parent's account, free on the Standard plan. Read our full [Revolut review](/en/blog/revolut-review-2026) for the details.

**Best for:** travel spending and students under 18.

## Wise — the best for money from home

Wise is not a bank — it is the cheapest way to receive money from family abroad. The account is free, you get a Belgian BE IBAN and local bank details in 40+ currencies, and transfers use the real mid-market exchange rate with a transparent fee of typically 0.3–0.7%. The debit card costs a one-time €7–10. You must be [at least 18 years old](https://wise.com/ph/legal/terms-of-use-en) to use Wise.

The trade-off is protection: Wise is an electronic money institution, so your balance is safeguarded in separate accounts but **not** covered by the EU deposit guarantee of up to €100,000. Use Wise to receive and convert money, then move what you need into your main bank account — do not park your savings there. See our [Wise review](/en/blog/wise-review-2026) for the full picture.

**Best for:** receiving regular transfers from family outside the eurozone.

## Trade Republic — the best for savings and first investing

Trade Republic is the odd one out: a broker that is also a full German bank. It pays [2.50% a year on all uninvested cash](https://finanzaseu.com/en/blog/trade-republic-review-2026), and 4.00% for new clients on up to €20,000 until 1 March 2027 in several countries including Spain, Italy, the Netherlands and Portugal (in Germany the new-client rate is 2.50%). There is no monthly fee, and bank-held cash is covered by the German deposit guarantee up to €100,000. You must be [at least 18](https://northern.finance/en/review/trade-republic-account/) and resident in one of the 18 countries it serves.

It also works as a first broker: €1 per trade, and free ETF savings plans from €1 a month. The debit card has no subscription fee, and free ATM withdrawals start from €100. What it is not: a travel card — there is no free currency-exchange allowance, so pair it with Revolut or Wise for spending outside the eurozone. Full details in our [Trade Republic review](/en/blog/trade-republic-review-2026).

**Best for:** students with savings, or starting monthly ETF investing for €0.

## DKB — the best traditional option in Germany

If you prefer a conventional German bank to a fintech app, DKB is the strongest choice: the current account is [free for customers under 28](https://www.workingstudentjobs.de/blog/student-discounts-germany), with a free Visa debit card, free SEPA transfers and no income requirement. You get a German IBAN and the German deposit guarantee up to €100,000. The trade-off is speed: opening takes longer than the 10-minute fintech sign-ups.

**Best for:** students in Germany who want a traditional bank, free until 28.

## The IBAN trap: your landlord may not accept your account

Under EU rules, every euro IBAN must be treated equally — a Lithuanian or Belgian IBAN is legally as valid as a German one for rent, salary and direct debits. In practice, some landlords, employers and universities still refuse non-local IBANs. That is why the IBAN column in the table matters: if your landlord, university or employer insists on a local IBAN, open N26, DKB or bunq (signing up as a resident of your study country) for rent and salary, and keep Revolut or Wise for spending and transfers. We explain your rights in our guide to [IBAN discrimination in the EU](/en/blog/iban-discrimination-eu-consumer-rights-2026).

## Under 18? Your options are narrower

N26, bunq, Wise and Trade Republic all require 18+. If you are 16 or 17, Revolut lets you open your own account in Ireland and the UK with some features switched off, and Revolut Kids & Teens covers ages 6–17 through a parent. Otherwise, a traditional bank account opened with a parent is the route. Our guide to the [best bank accounts for teens in Europe](/en/blog/best-bank-accounts-teens-kids-europe-2026) covers the options by age.

## Non-EU students in Germany: the blocked account

If you need a German student visa, you also need a blocked account (*Sperrkonto*) proving you can support yourself. For 2026 the German government requires [€11,904 for the year — €992 a month](https://www.study-abroad.org/blog/student-bank-account-europe/) — locked in the blocked account and released to you monthly. Open the blocked account first, then a current account alongside it for daily life: bunq if your passport is on N26's rejected list, N26 if it is accepted and you want a permanently free account.

## Verdict: the two-account setup wins

For most students, the winning combination is two free accounts: one with a local IBAN for rent and salary, one for foreign currency and travel.

- **In Germany, aged 25 or under?** bunq Pro free is the best deal here — a full account with a German IBAN for €0.
- **One free account for anywhere in Europe?** N26 Standard: free forever, no age cutoff, German IBAN.
- **Travel a lot or receive money from outside the eurozone?** Add Revolut (free FX up to €1,000 a month) or Wise (cheapest large transfers).
- **Have savings or want to start investing?** Trade Republic pays 2.50% on your cash and lets you automate ETF investing from €1 for free.

Whatever you choose, open the account before you travel if you can — N26, Revolut, Wise and bunq all let you sign up from your home country with just a passport — and always confirm the current fees on the provider's own site before applying.
