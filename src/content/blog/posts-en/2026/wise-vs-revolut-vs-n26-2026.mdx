# Wise vs Revolut vs N26 in 2026: Fees, FX & Which One Wins

Wise, Revolut and N26 are the three apps most Europeans reach for when a traditional bank feels too slow. They look similar — a slick app, a debit card, no branch — but they solve different problems. Wise is a money-transfer specialist built around exchange rates. Revolut is an all-in-one app. N26 is the closest thing to a normal bank, on a phone.

Pick the wrong one and you pay for it: a weekend exchange markup here, a 1.7% fee on foreign cash withdrawals there, or savings earning a fraction of what they could. Fees and rates below were checked against the providers' own pages and independent reviews in October–November 2026. Revolut and N26 price some plans by country — figures are for eurozone customers, mainly the Netherlands, unless stated otherwise.

## What each one actually is — and why it matters

The most important difference is legal, not cosmetic. **N26 is a full German bank** supervised by BaFin, and **Revolut's European accounts sit with Revolut Bank UAB**, a Lithuanian bank supervised by the Bank of Lithuania and the ECB. Deposits at both are covered by their national deposit guarantee schemes up to €100,000. **Wise is not a bank**: Wise Europe SA is a payment institution supervised by the National Bank of Belgium, and customer money is safeguarded in segregated accounts — but it is not covered by any deposit guarantee scheme. [Freenance's comparison](https://freenance.io/products/best-multi-currency-accounts-europe-2026/), checked 26 October 2026, lays out the three licences side by side. Safeguarding means your money is kept separate from Wise's own funds and should be returned if the company fails — but it is not the same legal guarantee as a bank deposit. That is why most Wise users keep their salary and emergency fund in a bank, and use Wise for moving and holding foreign currency.

## Exchange rates and conversion fees

This is where the three diverge most, and where the "cheapest" answer depends on how much you convert and when.

**Wise** converts at the mid-market rate — the rate you see on Google — and adds a visible fee that starts at 0.2% and varies by currency pair. For EUR to USD it was 0.46% in October 2026, or €4.58 per €1,000, according to [EU Personal Finance](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut). There is no weekend markup and no monthly allowance: the fee is the whole price, seven days a week. Sending €1,000 to pounds cost €5.76 on 26 October 2026 in [Freenance's check](https://freenance.io/products/best-multi-currency-accounts-europe-2026/).

**Revolut** gives Standard customers €1,000 a month of currency exchange at the Revolut rate with no fee on weekdays. Above €1,000, a 1% fair-usage fee applies (some country fee sheets list 0.5%). The trap is the weekend: from Friday 17:00 to Sunday 18:00 New York time, Revolut adds a 1% markup on Standard and 0.5% on Plus. Premium (€10.99/month in the Netherlands) and above remove both. Plan prices and allowances come from [Revolut's own Netherlands plan page](https://revolut.com/en-NL/our-pricing-plans).

**N26** is a euro account, not a multi-currency one: you cannot hold USD or GBP balances. Card payments in other currencies use the Mastercard rate with no markup from N26 on any plan — no monthly limit, no weekend fee. The 1.7% fee applies only to cash withdrawals from ATMs outside the eurozone on Standard and Smart; Go and Metal waive it. For card spending abroad, N26 Standard is the cheapest of the three; for foreign cash, it is the most expensive unless you upgrade.

**The maths:** converting €1,500 to dollars on a Saturday costs about €6.87 at Wise (0.46%, any day), versus €15 at Revolut Standard (1% weekend markup) — but €0 at Revolut if you exchange on a weekday within your allowance. For a large one-off conversion, Wise's transparent percentage usually wins; for small weekday amounts, Revolut Standard is free.

## IBAN: where your account "lives"

Your IBAN's country code matters: some employers, landlords and tax offices still reject foreign IBANs, despite EU rules against IBAN discrimination.

- **Wise** gives you a **Belgian IBAN** for euros, plus local account details in 22 currencies — a US routing number, a UK sort code and others.
- **Revolut** issues a **Lithuanian IBAN** by default for EEA customers, but now offers local IBANs in several countries, including Spain, Portugal (branch operating since July 2025), Poland and the Nordic countries, per [Farol7](https://farol7.pt/en/finance/wise-n26-revolut-bunq-travel-accounts) and [Freenance](https://freenance.io/products/best-multi-currency-accounts-europe-2026/).
- **N26** issues a **German IBAN** in most countries, with French, Italian and Spanish IBANs for residents of those countries.

If your employer or landlord insists on a local IBAN, Revolut's expanding local branches and N26's DE/FR/IT/ES IBANs have the edge. If you get paid in dollars or pounds, Wise's local account details in 22 currencies are unmatched here.

## Cards and ATM withdrawals

All three offer a free virtual card for Apple Pay and Google Pay; physical cards cost €7 (Wise), €7.99 delivery (Revolut) or €10 (N26), all one-off.

ATM allowances are tight on every free plan:

- **Wise:** €250 a month free, then 2.69% on the amount above it.
- **Revolut Standard:** €200 or 5 withdrawals per rolling month, then 2% per withdrawal (minimum €1). Paid plans raise the limit to €400 (Premium), €800 (Metal) and €2,000 (Ultra).
- **N26 Standard:** 2 free withdrawals a month in the eurozone, then €2 each. Smart gets 3, Go 5 and Metal 8, with Go and Metal adding unlimited free withdrawals outside the eurozone. Standard and Smart also pay a 1.7% fee on withdrawals outside the eurozone.

ATM operators can add their own fee on top. If you withdraw a lot of cash abroad, Revolut Metal's €800 or N26 Go's unlimited non-eurozone withdrawals are the only plans here that solve it.

## Interest on your balance

**Wise Interest** pays 2.25% on euro balances (as of 23 September 2026), but it is an investment in a money market fund run with BlackRock, not a deposit: your capital is at risk and the rate is variable. **Revolut's Instant Access Savings** pays 2.50% a year up to €2,500 and 1.25% above that on the Standard plan in the Netherlands, per [Revolut's plan page](https://revolut.com/en-NL/our-pricing-plans) — a real savings rate, though the higher tier is capped. **N26's Instant Savings** pays just 0.30% a year on Standard and Smart, 0.50% on Go and 1.50% on Metal — the weakest rates here, and the best needs Metal. N26's alternative, the Flexible Cash Fund, offered up to 2.63% a year as of 18 November 2026, but like Wise Interest it is a fund investment with capital at risk, with service fees from 1 January 2027 (0% for Metal, up to 0.30% for Standard), per [N26's own site](https://n26.com/en-eu/metal).

For a €2,500 buffer, Revolut Standard's 2.50% is the best straightforward rate here. For larger sums, none of the three replaces a dedicated savings account.

## Who each one is best for

**Travelling: Revolut (Premium), Wise or N26 — depending on how you pay.** For a short trip, Revolut Standard is hard to beat if you exchange on weekdays within the €1,000 allowance — free FX, free card, €200 of free ATM cash. For longer trips, weekend spending or larger amounts, Wise's flat 0.46%-style fee with no weekend markup is simpler and often cheaper. N26 Standard is the best of the three if you pay by card — no markup, no weekend fee, no monthly limit — but the weakest if you rely on foreign cash, where the 1.7% ATM fee applies; the travel perks only start at Go (€9.90/month).

**Expat life: Wise + one bank.** No single account covers the expat case. Wise wins for receiving a salary or freelance income in another currency — local account details in 22 currencies, mid-market rate, no monthly fee. Pair it with Revolut or N26 for daily spending, direct debits and deposit-protected savings in euros.

**Daily banking: N26 or Revolut.** If you want one account for salary, bills and card spending in euros, N26 is the most bank-like: a German IBAN, a full banking licence, sub-accounts ("Spaces") for budgeting, and no FX complexity. Revolut matches it on banking basics and adds budgeting tools, instant transfers to other Revolut users and a much bigger feature set — at the cost of a Lithuanian IBAN by default and a plan structure that nudges you toward paid tiers.

**Holding multiple currencies: Wise, clearly.** Holding 40+ currencies with local account details is Wise's core product. Revolut holds 30+ currencies and is a good second choice, especially on paid plans. N26 does not do this at all — it is a euro-only account.

## The verdict

There is no single winner, because the three are not the same product. **Wise** is the best tool for moving, receiving and holding foreign currency, with the most transparent pricing and no weekend surprises — but it is not a bank, and your money has no deposit guarantee. **Revolut** is the best all-rounder: a real bank with €100,000 deposit protection, free weekday exchange up to €1,000 a month, and the best savings rate on a small balance — as long as you dodge the weekend markup and the fair-usage limit. **N26** is the best pure euro bank account of the three, with a German IBAN and the simplest experience — and its free plan is the cheapest card for foreign card payments, though a poor one for foreign cash withdrawals, and its savings rates lag unless you pay for Metal.
