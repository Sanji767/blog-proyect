# Best High-Yield Savings Accounts in Europe (2026): Top Rates Compared

Savings rates in Europe have moved twice this year — and in the direction savers like. The European Central Bank raised its deposit facility rate to 2.25% in June 2026 and again to 2.50% in September, and held it there on 29 October 2026. The next decision is 17 December 2026. The best flexible accounts now pay around 2.50% to 2.60%, and fixed-term deposits reach 3.45% for one year. ([Source: ECB interest rates](https://euribor-rates.info/en/ecb-interest-rates/))

The gap between the best and the worst accounts is now worth real money. On a €10,000 emergency fund, the difference between a high-street account paying near 0% and a top flexible account at 2.50% is €250 a year, before tax — for the same money, the same deposit protection, and no lock-in. This guide compares the best-paying options in Europe in November 2026, with the conditions and the catches.

## The Best Options in Detail

### 1. Trade Republic — 2.50% on All Cash, 4% for New Clients

Trade Republic pays 2.50% a year (2.53% AER) on your entire uninvested balance, with no cap and no lock-in. Interest accrues daily and is paid monthly, and you can withdraw at any time without penalty. New clients in Portugal, Spain, Italy, the Netherlands, Austria, Belgium, Ireland, Finland and Slovenia earn 4.00% (4.07% AER) on up to €20,000 until 1 March 2027; in Germany the rate is 2.50% for everyone. The standard rate rose from 2.25% on 16 September 2026, when the ECB raised its deposit rate. ([Source: EU Personal Finance, Trade Republic review](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/trade-republic-review))

Trade Republic is a fully licensed German bank, so cash balances are covered by the German deposit guarantee up to €100,000. The practical catch is the promotional cliff: after 1 March 2027, new clients drop to the standard 2.50% — still the best uncapped flexible rate on this list, but budget for the lower figure.

### 2. Scalable Capital — 2.60% Flexible, 2.75% Fixed for 6 Months

Scalable Capital, Germany's other big neobroker-turned-bank, pays 2.60% a year in its Overnight account — currently the highest standard flexible rate we found — with no minimum deposit and interest calculated daily and paid monthly. Its 6-month fixed-term account pays 2.75%. Both figures were checked on the provider's own pages in early November 2026. ([Source: My Passive Income, EUR savings compared](https://my-passive-income.eu/eur-savings-accounts-compared-flexible-vs-fixed-term-rates-in-2026/))

Scalable has no debit card with cashback and no Saveback equivalent, so it is a pure savings play rather than an everyday account. If you only want the highest flexible rate and nothing else, it edges out Trade Republic by 0.10 percentage points — €10 a year on €10,000.

### 3. Raisin — 2.45% Flexible, Up to 3.45% Fixed for 1 Year

Raisin is not a bank but a marketplace: one account gives you access to savings products from partner banks across Europe, each covered by its own country's deposit guarantee up to €100,000. As of 17 November 2026, its best flexible partner-bank account paid 2.45%, and its best 1-year fixed deposit paid 3.45% — against 2.86% at NIBC and 2.25% at ABN AMRO for the same term. Longer terms pay more: 3.50% for 2–3 years, 3.85% for 5 years. ([Source: Expat Netherlands Hub, savings rates November 2026](https://expatnetherlandshub.com/guides/finance/best-savings-account-netherlands/))

Raisin also runs a Starter Account for new customers paying 3.10% AER, fixed for three months on up to €100,000, with withdrawals allowed without penalty. After three months the rate drops to the standard flexible rate, so treat it as a bonus, not a plan. The trade-off for the higher fixed rates is obvious: your money is locked for the term, and early withdrawal is usually impossible.

### 4. bunq Savings — 1.61% Base, 3.11% Bonus

bunq's savings account pays a 1.61% base rate, with a 3.11% bonus rate on the part of your balance above a threshold that depends on your own balance history, credited weekly. It is available on every plan, including the free one — but the bonus structure means your effective rate depends on how much you hold and for how long, so it is harder to predict than a flat rate. ([Source: Freenance, bunq Savings Review](https://freenance.io/savings/bunq-easy-savings-2026-review-3-percent-eur-deposit-protection/))

bunq is a licensed Dutch bank, so deposits are covered up to €100,000 by the Dutch scheme. The savings account works best as a complement to bunq's budgeting sub-accounts rather than as a standalone rate-chaser.

### 5. N26 Instant Savings — 0.30% to 1.50%, Depending on Your Plan

N26's Instant Savings account pays interest based on your plan: 0.30% a year on Standard and Smart, 0.50% on Go and 1.50% on Metal. Interest is calculated daily and paid monthly. Even the Metal rate trails the market leaders, and it requires a €16.90/month subscription — you would need a large balance for the extra interest to cover the fee. N26 also offers a Flexible Cash Fund paying up to 2.63% a year, but that is a money market fund, not a deposit: returns vary daily and it is not covered by the deposit guarantee. ([Source: N26](https://n26.com/en-eu/expats-bank-account))

N26 is a fine everyday bank, but it is not where your savings should live.

### 6. Trading 212 — 2.50% on Uninvested EUR Cash

Trading 212 pays 2.50% on uninvested EUR cash on Trading 212 Markets Ltd accounts, with rates varying by entity and currency. The money sits in your Invest account and can be spent directly with the 212 debit card, which makes it unusually liquid for an interest-bearing balance. The important distinction: Trading 212 is an investment firm, not a bank, so cash is covered by investor protection schemes rather than a bank deposit guarantee. ([Source: EU Personal Finance, Trading 212 vs Trade Republic](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/trading-212-vs-trade-republic))

## Flexible vs Fixed-Term: Which Should You Choose?

- **Emergency fund (3–6 months of expenses):** flexible, always. You need the money instantly, and the 0.85-point gap between the best flexible rate (2.60%) and the best 1-year fixed rate (3.45%) is worth €85 a year on €10,000 — cheap insurance against needing the cash during the term.
- **Money you will not need for a year or more:** fixed-term deposits. Raisin's 3.45% for one year locks in today's rate, which also protects you if the ECB cuts rates at a future meeting.
- **A large lump sum:** split it. Keep the emergency portion flexible and ladder the rest across 6-month, 1-year and 2-year terms so some money frees up regularly.

## How to Choose

- **Highest rate with instant access:** Scalable Capital Overnight at 2.60%, or Trade Republic at 2.50% if you also want a card and investing.
- **Highest rate overall:** a 1-year fixed deposit via Raisin at 3.45%.
- **New customer bonus:** Trade Republic's 4% until 1 March 2027 (up to €20,000, selected countries) or Raisin's 3.10% Starter rate for three months.
- **Simplest setup:** Trade Republic or bunq — one app, no separate savings marketplace, no lock-in.

## Deposit Protection: What €100,000 Really Covers

Every licensed bank on this list is backed by an EU Deposit Guarantee Scheme: if the bank fails, your deposits are protected up to €100,000 per depositor, per institution. With Raisin, the guarantee comes from the partner bank's country, not from Raisin itself — so check which bank actually holds your deposit, and keep your total below €100,000 per bank if you hold several products.

What the guarantee does not cover matters just as much: money market funds (such as N26's Flexible Cash Fund and Wise Interest), investments, and e-money balances at non-bank providers. A 2.63% fund return is not the same as a 2.50% deposit rate, because only one of them is guaranteed.

## Downsides and Things to Watch

- **Flexible rates follow the ECB.** The 2.50% rates on this list exist because the ECB deposit rate is 2.50%. If the Governing Council cuts rates at its 17 December 2026 meeting, flexible rates will fall within weeks. Fixed-term deposits are the only way to lock a rate in.
- **Promotional rates expire.** Trade Republic's 4% ends 1 March 2027; Raisin's Starter rate lasts three months. Always note the rate that applies afterwards.
- **Interest is taxable.** In most EU countries, interest on savings is taxed as income or capital gains, and some providers withhold tax at source while others do not. The rates in this guide are gross, before tax.
- **Chasing the last 0.1% has a cost.** Opening a fifth account for an extra €10 a year adds paperwork, tax reporting and another app to monitor. Two good accounts beat five marginally better ones.
