# Revolut Review 2026: Is It Safe? Fees, Plans & Honest Verdict

Revolut is now the biggest digital bank in Europe by customers: [more than 80 million retail customers worldwide](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/international/4001620-graphic-revoluts-rise-to-become-europes-115-billion-big-bank-rival?amp) as of August 2026, and a $115 billion private valuation confirmed in November 2026. It started in 2015 as a travel card with good exchange rates; today it is a full bank in the EU, with savings, investments, crypto, insurance and a subscription ladder from free to €65 a month. For the key facts at a glance, see the [Revolut profile](/en/programas/revolut) — fees, IBAN, cards and how to open an account — or the head-to-head [Revolut vs N26](/en/comparar/revolut-vs-n26) duel.

This review uses Revolut's official Netherlands pricing, checked on 20 November 2026. **Prices vary by country of residence** — they are not the same across the eurozone. Where the difference matters, we flag it. Always confirm the price for your own country in the app before subscribing.

For comparison, the same plans in Spain cost €3.99 (Plus), €9.99 (Premium), €15.99 (Metal) and €55 (Ultra) — the Netherlands is one of the more expensive markets in the eurozone.

What each paid plan adds beyond the limits above:

- **Plus (€3.99):** personalised card, purchase protection, priority support, 1 RevPoint per €10 spent.
- **Premium (€10.99):** global medical insurance, personal liability cover up to €1m a year, 5 commission-free stock trades a month, eSIM data, discounted lounge access, 1 RevPoint per €4 spent.
- **Metal (€19.99):** 18 g steel card, 10 commission-free trades a month, more eSIM data, trip cancellation and winter sports cover, 1 RevPoint per €2 spent.
- **Ultra (€65):** unlimited lounge access, platinum-plated card, monthly global data, cancellation cover, Trading Pro included, 1 RevPoint per €1 spent.

## Currency exchange: the rules that actually matter

Revolut's exchange rates are the reason most people open the account, and the rules are plan-dependent. Get them wrong and the "free" account stops being free.

- **Standard:** the first €1,000 of currency exchange per rolling month is free on weekdays. Above that, a 1% fair-usage fee applies.
- **Plus:** €3,000 a month free, then 0.5%.
- **Premium, Metal and Ultra:** unlimited exchange with no fair-usage fee.
- **Weekend fee:** exchanges made between 17:00 Friday and 18:00 Sunday New York time carry an extra fee — 1% on Standard, 0.5% on Plus, and nothing on Premium and above.

Two details worth knowing. First, Revolut uses its own exchange rate, set from independent data feeds — it is not guaranteed to be the mid-market rate the way Wise's is, though in practice it is usually very close on major currencies on weekdays. Second, the weekend window is measured in New York time, so in Europe it runs from Friday 23:00 to Monday 00:00 CET in winter — most of your Saturday and Sunday spending abroad falls inside it on the free plan.

The practical takeaway: Standard is genuinely free for occasional travel, but if you regularly convert more than €1,000 a month or mostly travel at weekends, Premium's €10.99 pays for itself in avoided fees.

## ATM withdrawals and the card

The first physical card is free on every plan, but delivery is not: local standard delivery costs €7.99 in the Netherlands, local express €19.99, and a replacement card is €6 plus delivery. Virtual and disposable virtual cards — single-use numbers for online shopping — are free and unlimited.

Free ATM allowances per rolling month, from [Revolut's help pages](https://help.revolut.com/en-NL/help/card-payments-withdrawals/atm-withdrawals/withdrawing-in-other-currencies/):

- **Standard:** €200 or 5 withdrawals, whichever comes first
- **Plus:** €200
- **Premium:** €400
- **Metal:** €800
- **Ultra:** €2,000

After the allowance, Revolut charges 2% of the withdrawal amount, with a €1 minimum per withdrawal. Note the Standard plan's double limit: five small withdrawals can exhaust your free allowance even if you have only taken out €100. The ATM's own operator may add a fee on top, which Revolut does not control.

## Savings: Instant Access Savings

Revolut's savings account pays daily interest with instant access and no minimum deposit. Rates are variable and depend on your plan. The figures below are from Revolut's [pre-contractual information for the Netherlands](https://www.revolut.com/en-NL/legal/pre-contractual-information-instant-access-savings-account/), updated 15 October 2026:

| Plan | EUR savings rate (variable, p.a.) |
|---|---|
| Standard | 2.50% up to €2,500, then 1.25% |
| Plus | 2.50% up to €2,500, then 1.25% |
| Premium | 2.50% up to €5,000, then 1.50% |
| Metal | 2.50% |
| Ultra | 2.75% up to €100,000, then 2.50% |

The maximum eligible deposit is €5,000,000. The tiered structure matters: on Standard, only your first €2,500 earns the headline 2.50% — everything above it earns 1.25%, so the effective rate on a €10,000 balance is under 1.6%. The savings account is a real bank deposit with Revolut Bank UAB, so unlike Wise Interest it is covered by the deposit guarantee (see the safety section below). Rates differ by country: in Spain, for example, the same account pays 1.15% TAE on Standard and up to 2.27% TAE on Ultra.

## Crypto, stocks and investments

Revolut packs a broker into the banking app: stocks and ETFs, crypto, commodities and a robo-advisor. Investment services in the EEA are provided by Revolut Securities Europe UAB, authorised and regulated by the Bank of Lithuania. Capital is at risk.

**Stocks and ETFs.** Commission-free trades depend on your plan: 5 a month on Premium, 10 a month on Metal and Ultra; Standard and Plus include none on the current Netherlands plan page. Beyond the allowance, each executed order costs €1. Ultra includes Trading Pro, Revolut's advanced trading platform.

**Crypto.** Revolut's crypto fees are plan-based and volume-based, per its [official crypto fee schedule](https://cdn.revolut.com/terms_and_conditions/pdf/personal_fees_exchanging_cryptocurrencies_e8264c8b_1.0.11_1793812975_en.pdf): 1.49% per exchange on Standard and Plus, 0.99% on Premium and Metal, and 0.49% on Ultra for the lowest volume tier (under €10,000 in 30 days), falling to 0% at very high volumes. Minimum fees apply to small trades under €200 — for example, €0.99 to €2.99 depending on the amount on Standard. These fees are high compared with a dedicated exchange.

**The honest assessment:** Revolut is a convenient place to make your first investments, but it is not the cheapest broker in Europe. If you trade more than a few times a month, a low-cost broker will beat it on fees.

## Is Revolut safe?

Yes — with the important qualification that applies to every bank.

Revolut Bank UAB is a licensed bank, authorised and regulated by the Bank of Lithuania and the European Central Bank. In the Netherlands it operates through a local branch supervised by the Dutch central bank (DNB), and Dutch residents now receive a Dutch IBAN. Deposits are protected up to **€100,000 per person** under the Lithuanian deposit guarantee scheme — the same EU-wide limit as any other European bank, just administered by Lithuania rather than the Netherlands.

That is a genuine difference from Wise, which is a payment institution with safeguarding but no deposit guarantee. It is also why Revolut can be a primary account in a way Wise cannot: salary, direct debits, iDEAL (Revolut is on the iDEAL issuer list) and a Dutch IBAN all work.

The caveats are operational, not structural. Revolut's customer support is primarily in-app chat, and account freezes for compliance checks — while far less common than a few years ago — still generate the bulk of complaints. The company is profitable (pre-tax profit of £1.7 billion in 2025) and holds banking licences in the UK (full licence since March 2026), the EU, Mexico, Australia and New Zealand, with conditional approval for a US national bank charter. It is no longer a startup running on venture money.

## Pros and cons

**Pros:**

- Real bank with a Dutch IBAN, iDEAL and €100,000 deposit protection
- Free Standard plan with €1,000/month free currency exchange on weekdays
- Savings account with daily interest and instant access, up to 2.75% p.a. on Ultra
- Disposable virtual cards, instant transfers between Revolut users, strong app
- Travel insurance, lounge access and eSIM data bundled into the higher plans
- 80 million+ customers and a profitable, well-capitalised business

**Cons:**

- Weekend FX surcharge (1%) and the €1,000 fair-usage cap bite on the free plan
- Crypto fees (1.49% on Standard) are expensive versus a dedicated exchange
- No commission-free stock trades on Standard and Plus in the Netherlands
- Prices vary by country and rise without much notice — the Netherlands pays more than Spain or Germany
- Support is chat-first; complex cases can be slow to resolve
- Ultra at €65 a month is hard to justify for almost anyone

## Verdict

Revolut Standard is the best free travel-and-spending account in Europe for most people: free card, free SEPA payments, €1,000 a month of free currency exchange and a savings account that pays a real rate. Premium at €10.99 a month is the sweet spot for frequent travellers — unlimited exchange, no weekend markup, travel insurance and a higher savings rate repay the subscription quickly if you actually travel.

Metal and Ultra are lifestyle purchases, not financial ones: the maths only works if you use the lounge access, the insurance and the eSIM data every month. And if your main need is converting large amounts or getting paid in several currencies, Wise still does that one job better. The smart setup for many Europeans is both: Revolut as the everyday account, Wise for currency.
