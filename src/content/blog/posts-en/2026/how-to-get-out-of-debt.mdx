# How to Get Out of Debt Fast

Debt can feel overwhelming, but following a structured plan can help you become debt-free faster than you think. You are not alone in this: in 2025, [9.2% of people in the EU were in arrears](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Quality_of_life_indicators_-_economic_security_and_physical_safety) on mortgage or rent, utility bills or hire purchase payments, according to Eurostat. The difference between people who escape debt and people who stay stuck is rarely income — it is having a plan and sticking to it.

## Why expensive debt is the emergency, not the inconvenience

Not all debt costs the same. A mortgage at 3–4% is a manageable tool; revolving credit card debt is a fire. According to the ECB's bank interest rate statistics for September 2026, the average rates on household borrowing in the euro area were:

| Type of borrowing | Average rate (Sep 2026) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Extended credit card credit | 16.52% |
| New consumer loans | 7.82% |
| Overdrafts and revolving loans | 7.35% |
| New home loans | 3.62% |

*Source: [ECB bank interest rate statistics](https://data.ecb.europa.eu/main-figures/bank-interest-rates/loans), September 2026.*

At 16.52%, a €3,000 credit card balance costs roughly €496 a year in interest alone — before you have repaid a single euro of the balance itself. Meanwhile, the same ECB data shows overnight deposits paying just 0.30%. Every euro sitting in a current account while a card balance revolves is a euro working against you. That is why clearing high-interest debt comes before saving and investing in any sensible order of operations.

## Step 1: List All Your Debts

Create a simple spreadsheet detailing every debt you owe — no exceptions, no rounding down:

- Total balance owed
- Minimum monthly payment
- Annual Percentage Rate (APR)
- Payment due date
- Whether the rate is fixed or variable

Include everything: credit cards, personal loans, overdrafts, store cards, buy-now-pay-later balances, and money owed to family. Debts you leave off the list are debts your plan cannot fix. If you are unsure about a balance or rate, check your latest statement or your banking app — never guess.

## Step 2: Stop the Bleeding

Before you attack the balances, stop them growing:

- **Freeze new borrowing.** Remove saved card details from shopping apps and delete buy-now-pay-later options at checkout. If a card is the problem, freeze it in the app or cut it up — you can keep the account open for your credit history while making it impossible to spend on.
- **Build a €1,000 starter buffer first.** This sounds backwards, but without even a small cushion, the next car repair or broken appliance goes straight back onto a card and undoes your progress. Save €1,000, then throw everything else at the debt.
- **Switch to cash or debit for daily spending.** When the money visibly leaves your account, spending drops. Our guide to [building an emergency fund](/en/blog/how-to-build-emergency-fund-guide-2026) explains how to grow that starter buffer into a full 3–6 month cushion once the expensive debt is gone.

## Step 3: Choose Your Payoff Strategy

Pay the minimum on every debt, every month, without fail — missed payments mean fees and damage to your credit record. Then throw all your spare money at one target debt at a time. Which one? There are two proven methods.

### Option A: The Debt Avalanche Method (Mathematically Superior)

- Pay minimums on all debts.
- Throw all extra money at the debt with the **highest interest rate**.
- Saves the most money in interest over time.

With the ECB averages above, the avalanche order is clear: credit card (16.52%) first, then the personal loan (7.82%), then the overdraft (7.35%). On a €2,000 card balance, the avalanche saves you roughly €27 a month in interest charges versus leaving it to revolve — money that then accelerates the next debt.

### Option B: The Debt Snowball Method (Psychologically Superior)

- Pay minimums on all debts.
- Throw all extra money at the debt with the **smallest total balance**.
- Gives quick emotional wins to keep you motivated.

This is not just folk wisdom. Researchers at Northwestern University's Kellogg School of Management analysed nearly 6,000 people in a debt settlement programme and found that [closing individual accounts — regardless of the dollar balance — predicted successful debt elimination](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/News_Articles/2012/snowball-approach.aspx), while the total amount paid off did not. The "small victories" kept people in the programme long enough to finish.

### Which should you pick?

If your rates are far apart — say a 16.52% card next to a 7.35% overdraft — the avalanche saves real money and is the rational choice. If your rates are close together, the snowball costs little extra and the quick wins genuinely help you stick with the plan. The best method is the one you will still be following in six months.

## Step 4: Negotiate Lower Interest Rates

Call your card issuers or lenders and ask for a temporary interest rate reduction or balance transfer to a 0% APR card. This works more often than people expect, especially if you have a history of on-time payments. Before you call:

- Know your current rate and balance, and have a competing offer in mind.
- Ask specifically for a lower APR, a waived annual fee, or a hardship programme.
- If they refuse, ask what would qualify you in three months — then do that.

Balance transfers can help, but read the terms: the 0% period is usually 6–12 months, transfer fees of 1–3% are common, and any balance left when the promotional period ends reverts to the standard rate — which in the euro area averages 16.52% on credit cards. A transfer only helps if you have a plan to clear the balance inside the promotional window. Debt consolidation loans work the same way: they help only if the new rate is genuinely lower than the weighted average of what you are replacing, and only if you do not run the old cards back up.

## Step 5: Find the Extra Money

Most payoff plans fail on cash flow, not on willpower. The fastest wins:

- **Audit subscriptions.** Cancel anything you have not used in 30 days. A €15 monthly subscription is €180 a year — enough to clear a small store card.
- **Sell what you do not use.** One weekend of selling unused electronics, clothes and furniture can fund your starter buffer outright.
- **Redirect windfalls.** Tax refunds, bonuses and cashback go straight to the target debt, not to lifestyle.
- **Increase income, even temporarily.** Overtime, a weekend shift or freelance work for a few months can cut a year off a payoff plan. If you freelance, keep business money separate — our guide to the [best free business accounts for freelancers](/en/blog/best-free-business-accounts-freelancers-2026) covers accounts that cost €0 a month.

## Step 6: If the Debt Is Unmanageable, Get Help Early

If minimum payments already exceed what you can afford, or you are borrowing to pay other borrowing, do not wait for it to get worse. Every EU country has free, independent debt-advice services — your national consumer authority can point you to one — and lenders are generally required to work with borrowers in difficulty before taking enforcement action. A debt adviser can negotiate payment plans, freeze interest, and in some countries arrange a formal debt settlement. Asking for help is not failure; it is the fastest route out.

## FAQ

**Should I pay off debt or save first?**
Pay off anything above roughly 6–7% APR first, after building a €1,000 starter buffer. A savings account paying 2.50% cannot beat a card charging 16.52%. Once expensive debt is gone, build the full emergency fund, then invest — the full order is in our [saving vs. investing guide](/en/blog/saving-vs-investing-guide).

**Does paying off a card hurt my credit score?**
No. Paying a balance to zero lowers your credit utilisation, which helps. Closing a very old card can shorten your credit history, so keep the account open and the card frozen instead.

**How long does it take to get out of debt?**
It depends on the balance and the monthly surplus, not on the method. Someone with €5,000 of card debt paying €300 a month clears it in about 18 months; at €150 a month, roughly three years. The maths is unforgiving about one thing: paying only the minimum stretches a €3,000 card balance over a decade or more.
