# PSD3 and the PSR in 2026: Scam Refunds, IBAN Name Checks and What Changes for Your Money

Europe's new payments rulebook is nearly final. The Council adopted its first-reading position in late October 2026, the European Commission accepted the texts on 3 November 2026, and the European Parliament's plenary vote — indicatively scheduled for 14 December 2026 — is the last major step before publication, according to [Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/psd3-psr-explained/) and [Buckingham Capital Consulting](https://www.buckinghamcapitalconsulting.com/post/psd3-and-the-eu-payment-services-regulation-2026-what-payment-firms-need-to-prepare-for).

For your money, the headline changes are practical: a refund right when scammers impersonate your bank, an IBAN name check before every credit transfer, clearer ATM fees, cash in shops, and stricter fraud-monitoring duties. The catch is timing: most rules apply in late 2028, the full name check in 2029.

## Why PSD2 is being replaced

PSD2 dates from an era when fraud mostly meant stolen card details. Today's fraud tricks people into authorising the payment themselves. Payment fraud in the European Economic Area reached €4.2 billion in 2024, up from €3.5 billion in 2023, and users bore around 85% of credit transfer fraud losses, according to ECB and EBA figures reported by [Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/psd3-psr-explained/).

Around one in four people in the EU has been a victim of online fraud, and impersonation scams account for 22–25% of victims, according to EU Agency for Fundamental Rights (FRA) figures cited in the same analysis. In an impersonation scam, the criminal pretends to be your bank or the police and pressures you into moving money to a "safe" account. Banks often refuse refunds because you authorised the payment. The new rules are designed to close that gap.

## PSD3 vs PSR: two laws, one rulebook

The reform comes as two linked texts. PSD3 is a directive on authorisation and supervision: who may provide payment services, under what licence and with what capital. The PSR is a regulation on conduct: your day-to-day rights when you pay, dispute a transaction or connect an app to your account. Because a regulation applies directly across the EU, the PSR's consumer rules are meant to work the same way in every member state.

That split explains the timing. PSD3's licensing changes need national implementation, with existing licences running for up to 27 months; the PSR's conduct rules have a single EU-wide application date. When you read that "PSD3" gives you a refund right or a name check, it is usually the PSR doing the work.

## Scam refunds: the bank-impersonation rule

The most consequential change is a refund right for bank-impersonation fraud. If a scammer impersonates your payment provider and tricks you into authorising a payment, the agreed texts give you a right to a refund, subject to conditions in the final law, according to [Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/psd3-psr-explained/). This targets the current outcome in which users bear around 85% of credit transfer fraud losses because they pressed "confirm" themselves.

Two supporting rules give the right teeth. The burden of proof shifts to the provider: if you dispute a transaction, the firm must show the payment was properly authenticated and executed. And providers must monitor transactions in real time — including instant payments — to flag suspicious activity before money leaves. Online platforms and telecom operators also face fraud duties aimed at the channels scammers use, such as fake ads and spoofed numbers.

Not every loss will be repaid: the right is tied to provider impersonation and to conditions in the text, and prompt reporting still matters. But the starting point changes — the provider must show it monitored, warned and verified before it can refuse.

## IBAN name checks for every transfer

Before a credit transfer is executed, your provider must check that the name you entered matches the name on the destination IBAN and warn you if it does not. The duty will cover all credit transfers, and if the provider fails to check or to warn and you lose money as a result, liability follows, according to [Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/psd3-psr-explained/).

Part of this is already live. Since 9 October 2025, euro-area providers must offer instant euro credit transfers with verification of payee, instant payments may not cost more than ordinary ones, and funds must reach the recipient within 10 seconds, according to [GFM Review](https://www.gfmreview.com/fintech/europe-s-payment-stack-honestly). Payment and e-money institutions must send and receive instant payments by 9 April 2027. The PSR then extends the name check to every credit transfer, applying around 27 months after entry into force, in 2029.

## Fees, cash and clearer statements

- **ATM transparency.** Fees and exchange rates at cash machines must be shown upfront, so you can decline an expensive withdrawal instead of discovering the cost afterwards.
- **Cash in shops.** You will be able to withdraw up to €150 from a shop using chip and PIN, without making a purchase — useful as branches and ATMs thin out.
- **Recognisable statement names.** The merchant name on your statement must match the name the business trades under, ending the mystery debits that hide fraud.
- **No surcharging, extended.** The ban on surcharges for card payments extends to credit transfers and direct debits: the price you see is the price you pay.

## Open banking and strong authentication

Providers must offer dedicated interfaces (APIs) at least as good as their own apps, the old fallback mechanisms are removed, and screen scraping — where a third party logs in as you — is banned. In its place you get a permission dashboard to see which apps can access your account data and to withdraw access, according to [Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/psd3-psr-explained/).

Strong customer authentication must be free, must not depend solely on a smartphone, and must be accessible. If you do not own or cannot easily use a smartphone, your bank must offer another way to prove it is you. ## One licence for payment and e-money firms

PSD3 merges the payment institution and e-money institution licences into one and repeals EMD2, so wallets and payment firms operate under a single authorisation regime. Initial capital in the agreed text is €40,000 for money remittance, €50,000 for payment initiation, €150,000 for core payment services and €250,000 for e-money issuance, according to [Buckingham Capital Consulting](https://www.buckinghamcapitalconsulting.com/post/psd3-and-the-eu-payment-services-regulation-2026-what-payment-firms-need-to-prepare-for). Existing licences can run for up to 27 months during the transition. For consumers, the gain is consistency: the same standards whether your provider is a bank, a payment institution or an e-money firm.

## When each rule applies

- **Early 2027 (estimated):** PSD3 and the PSR enter into force, 20 days after Official Journal publication.
- **9 April 2027:** payment and e-money institutions must send and receive instant euro payments (under the separate Instant Payments Regulation, already in force).
- **Late 2028:** most PSR consumer rules apply, 21 months after entry into force — impersonation refunds, real-time monitoring, ATM transparency, shop cash and statement names.
- **2029:** verification of payee for all credit transfers (27 months); licence transition completes.

The digital euro is a separate project: Council position in December 2025, Parliament backed trilogues on 9 July 2026 (416–169), ECB selected 36 providers on 14 July 2026 for a 12-month pilot in late 2027, first issuance possible in 2029, according to [GFM Review](https://www.gfmreview.com/fintech/europe-s-payment-stack-honestly). Different law, different timeline.

## What to do now

1. **Use the name check you already have.** For instant euro payments it has been live since October 2025. Never override a mismatch warning without verifying through a channel you trust.
2. **Report scams to your bank and the police.** Prompt reporting and evidence — screenshots, numbers, messages — will still matter for any refund claim.
3. **Review app permissions.** Check which services can access your accounts and revoke anything you no longer use, ahead of the mandatory dashboard.
4. **Ask about non-app authentication.** If your bank only offers app approval, ask what alternatives exist today.

PSD3 and the PSR will not end payment fraud. What they change is who carries the loss when the system fails, and how much warning you get before money moves — a meaningful shift in the consumer's favour, arriving in stages from late 2028.
