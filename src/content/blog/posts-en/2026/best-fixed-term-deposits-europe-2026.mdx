# Best Fixed-Term Deposits in Europe 2026: Lock In Up to 3.55% Before the ECB Decides

Flexible savings accounts pay more than they did a year ago, but their rates move with every central-bank decision — and the next one is three weeks away. A fixed-term deposit does the opposite: you lock your money away for 3, 6 or 12 months, and the rate is guaranteed for the whole term, whatever the European Central Bank does on 17 December. With eurozone inflation back at 4% and a rate hike all but certain, that guarantee is suddenly worth something again.

The best 12-month euro deposits now pay around 3.5%, roughly a full percentage point above the average flexible account. On €10,000, that difference is about €100 a year. Here are the best rates available across Europe right now, the conditions attached to each, and how to decide whether to lock your money in today or wait for the ECB.

## Why fixed deposits matter again

The ECB has raised its deposit rate twice in 2026, from 2.00% to 2.50%, after the energy shock pushed eurozone inflation to 4.0% in October — the highest in three years, with energy prices about a fifth higher than a year earlier. Markets now treat another 25-basis-point hike on 17 December, to 2.75%, as near-certain: a [Reuters survey of 71 economists](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/the-euro-is-at-its-weakest-since-spring-2025-and-the-ecb-is-about-to-hike-202611171404) found all of them expecting it, and Bundesbank President Joachim Nagel said on 13 November that a further rise is highly probable. Lithuanian policymaker Gediminas Simkus went further on 23 November, telling Reuters that even a December hike "may not be enough to fully control inflation."

That creates a dilemma for savers. Flexible accounts will pay more if the ECB hikes — Trade Republic, for example, passes the deposit rate straight through, so its standard rate would rise from 2.50% toward 2.75%. But they will also pay less if rates fall, and every promotional rate on the market has an expiry date. A fixed deposit removes the guesswork: the rate you sign is the rate you get.

The gap is real. According to the ECB's own statistics on new deposit business for September 2026, the euro-area average for 1-to-2-year fixed deposits was 2.63%, with Lithuania topping the table at 3.60% — and the best individual offers beat even that. Flexible accounts from the big neobanks mostly pay 2.50% or less once their introductory offers expire.

## What a fixed deposit actually protects you from — and what it doesn't

The guarantee is the point. Once you sign, the bank cannot cut your rate, even if the ECB surprises everyone and holds or cuts on 17 December. If you believe rates have peaked — or you simply want certainty for money you will not need for a year — that is worth more than the extra half-point a promotional flexible account might offer for four months.

What it does not protect you from is inflation. Eurozone prices rose 4.0% in October, and no euro fixed deposit beats that before tax. A recent modelled comparison of where to park cash in France and Germany found that on €10,000 over a year, the best options left €250 in France and €196 in Germany — against the €340 and €280 needed just to keep pace with October prices. After tax, the gap is wider: Germany taxes savings interest at 26.375%, France at a flat rate of around 30%. A fixed deposit slows the erosion of your purchasing power; it does not stop it.

There is a second cost: your money is locked. Most fixed deposits pay nothing — or a penalty rate — if you withdraw early, and some do not allow early withdrawal at all. Never lock away your emergency fund or money you might need for a deposit, a tax bill or a move. The standard advice, three to six months of expenses in an instant-access account, still applies before a single euro goes into a fixed term.

## Lock in now, or wait for 17 December?

This is the question every saver in Europe is asking, and the honest answer depends on what you think the ECB will do after December. Three scenarios:

- **You lock in 3.55% for 12 months today.** If the ECB hikes on 17 December and again in 2027, flexible accounts will drift up toward 3% and you will have left some money on the table — but you will have earned 3.55% with zero effort and zero risk for the whole year.
- **You wait.** If the ECB hikes as expected, flexible rates rise and the fixed-deposit offers available in January may be slightly higher too. But promotional rates expire — Trading 212's 4.20% offer for new EU clients, for example, ends on 30 November — and a fixed deposit signed in January pays from January, not from today.
- **You split the difference.** This is what most savers should do: put half in a 12-month fixed deposit now and keep half flexible, then revisit after the December meeting. It is the only strategy that does not require predicting the ECB correctly.

One more consideration: the direction of travel. Simkus's warning that a December hike "may not be enough" and market pricing for one or two further hikes in 2027 both point the same way — rates are more likely to rise than fall in the next six months. That argues for shorter fixes (6 to 12 months rather than 3 to 5 years) so you can re-lock at higher rates later, and against locking everything away for years at today's levels. The 1822direkt curve tells the same story: 2.80% for 12 months, 3.10% for 36 months — the market is paying only 0.30 points extra for two more years of your money.

## How to open one safely

Four checks before you sign anything:

1. **Deposit protection.** Confirm the bank is covered by an EU deposit guarantee scheme up to €100,000 per depositor. Raisin partner banks are covered by their home country's scheme, not Germany's — that is normal, and the coverage is the same amount, but check the country.
2. **Who holds the money.** With a marketplace like Raisin, your deposit sits with the partner bank, not with Raisin itself. Read the partner bank's own terms, not just the marketplace summary.
3. **Early withdrawal.** Find the exact penalty before you commit. Some deposits allow early exit at a reduced rate; others do not allow it at all.
4. **Tax.** Interest is taxed where you live, not where the bank sits. Some countries withhold tax at source and you reclaim it; others pay gross and you declare it. Check your own country's rules — a 3.55% headline can be 2.60% after tax.

Fixed deposits will not make anyone rich. But for money that has to be safe — a house deposit, a tax reserve, next year's expenses — the difference between 2.50% flexible and 3.55% fixed is €105 a year on every €10,000, guaranteed. With inflation at 4% and the ECB about to move again, that guarantee is the best deal on the table for cautious savers.
