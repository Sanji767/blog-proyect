# 8 Best Digital Banks in Europe (2026): Revolut, N26, Wise Compared

Digital banking in Europe has completely transformed how expats, digital nomads, and European residents manage their money. Traditional brick-and-mortar banks with heavy monthly fees and slow customer service are rapidly losing ground to agile neobanks — and in 2026, the gap is wider than ever. Most of the accounts below cost €0 a month, open in minutes from your phone, and pay interest on your balance that high-street banks still refuse to match.

But "free" is never the whole story. Exchange limits, weekend markups, ATM fees and the difference between a licensed bank and an e-money institution all change which account is actually cheapest for you. In this guide, we compare the eight best digital banks in Europe for 2026 on fees, IBANs, exchange rates, savings interest and deposit protection — with every figure checked against the providers' own pricing pages in November 2026.

## 1. Revolut – Best Overall Multi-Currency Account

Revolut remains the most complete all-rounder: a European IBAN, a card, 38 currencies to hold and exchange, investing, crypto and savings in one app. The free Standard plan has no monthly fee, and weekday currency exchanges are free up to €1,000 a month. Above that limit, a 1% fair usage fee applies, and exchanges made at weekends (Friday 5pm to Sunday 6pm New York time) carry a 1% fee on Standard — a change that took effect in the terms applying from 15 October 2026. Crypto and commodities exchanges also count towards the €1,000 limit. ([Source: Revolut Standard fees](https://www.revolut.com/en-IT/legal/standard-fees/))

ATM withdrawals are free up to €200 or five withdrawals per rolling month, whichever comes first, then 2% (minimum €1). Savings interest on the Standard plan is 1.50%, rising to 2.25% on the Ultra plan. Revolut is a licensed bank (Revolut Bank UAB, Lithuania), so deposits are protected up to €100,000. ([Source: EU Personal Finance, Wise vs Revolut](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut))

**Best for:** travellers and expats who exchange under €1,000 a month and want one app for spending, saving and investing.

## 2. N26 – Best German IBAN and Deposit Protection

N26 is a fully licensed German bank with a free Standard account and a German IBAN — the detail that matters most if a landlord, employer or local authority insists on a domestic IBAN. Its standout feature is foreign exchange: N26 charges no markup on card payments in any currency, with no monthly limit, on any day of the week. Only the Mastercard exchange rate applies. ([Source: EU Personal Finance, N26 vs Revolut](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/n26-vs-revolut))

The plans are Standard (free), Smart (€4.90/month), Go (€9.90/month) and Metal (€16.90/month). The catch is cash: ATM withdrawals outside the eurozone cost 1.7% on Standard and Smart, and are free only on Go and Metal. Instant Savings pays just 0.30% a year on Standard and Smart, 0.50% on Go and 1.50% on Metal — the decent rate is locked behind the most expensive plan. ([Source: N26](https://n26.com/en-eu/expats-bank-account))

**Best for:** anyone who needs a German IBAN and pays by card abroad often, without exchanging large amounts in the app.

## 3. Wise – Best for International Transfers

Wise is not a bank — it is a payment institution — but for moving money across borders it is still the benchmark. It converts at the mid-market rate (the rate you see on Google) and shows its fee as a separate line before you confirm: from 0.2%, and 0.46% for EUR to USD as of October 2026, with no weekend markup at all. You get local account details to receive money in 22 currencies and a debit card that spends in more than 40. ([Source: EU Personal Finance, Wise vs Revolut](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut))

The account has no monthly fee; the physical card costs €7 one-off. ATM withdrawals are free up to €250 a month, then 2.69% on the excess. Because Wise is not a bank, your money is safeguarded in segregated accounts rather than covered by a deposit guarantee scheme — fine for a spending and transfer account, but not where you should park your life savings. ([Source: MoneyTransfer.store, Wise Card Review](https://www.moneytransfer.store/guides/wise-card-review))

**Best for:** freelancers paid in foreign currencies, and anyone sending money abroad or converting large amounts.

## 4. bunq – Best for Freelancers and Sub-accounts

bunq is a Dutch bank with a full banking licence, supervised by De Nederlandsche Bank, and its superpower is organisation: up to 25 sub-accounts, each with its own IBAN, on the Pro and Elite plans. The personal plans are Free (€0), Core (€3.99/month), Pro (€9.99/month) and Elite (€18.99/month), with 3, 5, 25 and 25 accounts respectively. ([Source: Bankverse, bunq Review 2026](https://www.bankverse.ch/bunq-review/))

Its savings account pays a 1.61% base rate, with a 3.11% bonus rate on balances above a threshold, credited weekly. The weak spots are foreign spending — Mastercard rate plus 0.5% on card payments outside the eurozone — and ATM fees: €2.99 per withdrawal on Free, with six free withdrawals a month only from Pro upwards. ([Source: Freenance, bunq Savings Review](https://freenance.io/savings/bunq-easy-savings-2026-review-3-percent-eur-deposit-protection/))

**Best for:** freelancers and budgeters who want a separate IBAN for tax, rent, holidays and shared costs.

## 5. Trade Republic – Best for Savings and Investing in One App

Trade Republic started as a broker and is now a full German bank — and in November 2026 it pays the highest straightforward rate on idle cash of any account on this list. Uninvested cash earns 2.50% a year as standard, with no balance limit, tracking the ECB deposit facility rate (2.50% since 16 September 2026). New clients in Spain, Portugal, Italy, the Netherlands, Austria, Belgium, Ireland, Finland and Slovenia earn 4.00% on up to €20,000 until 1 March 2027; in Germany the rate is 2.50% for everyone. Interest accrues daily and is paid monthly. ([Source: EU Personal Finance, Trade Republic review](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/trade-republic-review))

The Visa debit card has no monthly fee and no FX markup on card payments, and ATM withdrawals of €100 or more are free worldwide (€1 below €100). Card spending earns 1% Saveback, invested into an ETF savings plan of your choice, capped at €15 a month and requiring at least €50 a month in savings plans. ([Source: QuantRoutine, Trade Republic Review](https://quantroutine.com/brokers/trade-republic/))

**Best for:** savers and investors who want their emergency fund, ETF plans and everyday card in a single free app.

## 6. Vivid – Best for Cashback

Vivid is a German-founded fintech operating as Vivid Money S.A., a Luxembourg electronic money institution regulated by the CSSF — not a bank, so balances are safeguarded rather than covered by a deposit guarantee. Its pitch is cashback: up to 4% back on selected brands, with the option to receive rewards as fractional shares. ([Source: VCard Guide, Vivid Card Review 2026](https://www.vcardguide.com/vivid-card-review))

The Standard plan costs €4.90 a month, waived if you are an active customer — a combined balance of at least €3,000 across your Vivid accounts on the last day of the month, or at least €1,000 of card purchases in the previous month. Plus costs €6.90 and Prime €9.90 a month. The trade-off for travellers is a 1% markup on non-euro card payments, which makes it a poor primary travel card despite the cashback.

**Best for:** everyday euro spending where the cashback on selected brands outweighs the monthly fee.

## 7. C24 Bank – Best Free German Account With Interest

C24 is a fully licensed German bank (BaFin-supervised, deposits protected up to €100,000) offering a free Smart account with a German IBAN, a debit Mastercard and 0.75% a year on your checking balance up to €50,000, paid quarterly. The rate follows the ECB deposit facility rate minus 1.5 percentage points. Plus (€5.90/month) and Max (€9.90/month) add more free ATM withdrawals (20 a month, versus 4 on Smart) and more sub-accounts. ([Source: CheckAlle, C24 Bank Review 2026](https://checkalle.de/c24-bank-en/))

Two honest caveats: the app and website are German-only, and in November 2025 BaFin ordered anti-money-laundering measures at the bank and appointed a special representative. Neither affects deposit protection, but both are worth knowing before you switch.

**Best for:** residents of Germany who want a free local account that pays interest on the balance they actually use.

## 8. Openbank – Best Free Account Backed by a Big Bank

Openbank is the 100% digital bank of the Santander Group, and its Open Current Account is fee-free with no conditions: no maintenance, administration or closing fees, a free debit card, free SEPA transfers and free cash withdrawals at more than 5,000 Santander ATMs in Spain. You get a Spanish IBAN and deposits are covered up to €100,000 by the Spanish scheme. ([Source: ExpatMoneyEU, Best Bank Accounts in Spain](https://www.expatmoneyeu.com/best-bank-accounts-spain/))

The current account itself pays 0% interest, although new customers who bring their salary (at least €900 a month) and Bizum can get a separate remunerated account paying 3.25% TAE for one year. The main limitation is eligibility: you generally need Spanish residency and a NIE to open the account.

**Best for:** Spanish residents who want a genuinely free account with big-bank backing and a local IBAN.

## How to Choose the Right Digital Bank

- **For travel and FX:** Revolut (free within €1,000/month on weekdays) or Wise (predictable fee, no weekend markup). N26 is the dark horse: unlimited card payments at the Mastercard rate with no markup at all.
- **For salary and direct debits:** pick the IBAN your country expects — N26 or C24 for Germany, bunq for the Netherlands, Openbank for Spain. A local IBAN still avoids the occasional employer or landlord who rejects foreign ones.
- **For high-interest savings:** Trade Republic at 2.50% with no cap, or bunq's 3.11% bonus rate if your balance qualifies.
- **For freelancers and budgeting:** bunq's 25 IBANs, or N26 Spaces on the paid plans.
- **For cashback:** Vivid (up to 4% on selected brands) or Trade Republic's 1% Saveback.

Most people end up with two accounts: one local-IBAN bank for salary and bills, and one multi-currency account for travel and transfers. That combination costs €0 a month and covers every scenario on this list.

## Downsides and Things to Watch

- **Not all of these are banks.** Wise and Vivid are e-money institutions: your money is safeguarded in segregated accounts, but it is not covered by a deposit guarantee scheme. Keep your emergency fund at a licensed bank.
- **Free plans have limits that bite.** Revolut's €1,000 monthly exchange allowance, its 1% weekend fee and its five-withdrawal ATM cap can add up on a long trip — the paid plans exist precisely to remove them.
- **Interest rates are variable.** Trade Republic's 2.50% tracks the ECB deposit rate, which rose to 2.50% in September 2026 and could move again at the next decision on 17 December 2026. A rate quoted today is not a promise for next year.
- **Promotional rates expire.** Trade Republic's 4% new-client offer ends on 1 March 2027, and Openbank's 3.25% TAE lasts one year. Check what the account pays afterwards before you move your savings.
- **Support is app-first.** None of these banks has branches. If you value walking into an office to solve a problem, keep a traditional account alongside your digital one.
