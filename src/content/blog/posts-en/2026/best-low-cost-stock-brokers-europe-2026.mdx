# Best Low-Cost Brokers in Europe (2026): Cheap Stocks & ETFs, Compared

Long-term investing in stocks and Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) is one of the most effective ways to build wealth over time. But brokerage fees, custody charges and currency conversion costs quietly eat into returns — and the cheapest broker for a monthly ETF saver is not necessarily the cheapest for an active stock picker.

In this guide we compare the **top regulated low-cost brokers in Europe**, with fees, cash interest rates and investor protection verified in November 2026. All rates are variable and change with ECB policy: the ECB deposit facility rate stood at 2.50% in November 2026, which is why most brokers now pay around 2.50% on uninvested cash.

## In-Depth Review of the Top Platforms

### 1. Trade Republic — Best all-rounder for ETF savers

Trade Republic is a German bank and broker with a full banking licence, supervised by BaFin and the Bundesbank, available in 17 countries. Every trade costs **€1**, and automated savings plans in stocks and ETFs are **free from €1**. Uninvested cash earns **2.50% a year (2.53% AER)** with no balance limit as standard; new clients in Portugal, Spain, Italy, the Netherlands, Austria, Belgium, Ireland, Finland and Slovenia earn **4.00% (4.07% AER) on up to €20,000 until 1 March 2027** (in Germany the rate is 2.50% for everyone). Interest accrues daily and is paid monthly.

Cash held as a bank deposit is covered by the deposit guarantee up to €100,000. The debit card pays **1% Saveback** into an investment plan. The main limitation is that the 4% rate is a new-client offer with an end date (1 March 2027) that does not apply in Germany, and the standard rate tracks the ECB, so it will fall if the ECB cuts.

### 2. Trading 212 — Best truly commission-free trading

Trading 212 charges **zero commission** on stock and ETF trades, with no platform, custody or inactivity fees, and fractional shares from €1. The catch is the **0.15% FX fee** on trades in other currencies. Its Pies and AutoInvest feature automates portfolio building and rebalancing for free.

Interest on uninvested EUR cash depends on which entity holds your account: clients of **Trading 212 Markets Ltd** (CySEC) earn **2.50%**, while clients of **Trading 212 EU GmbH** (BaFin) earn **2.80%** from 1 November 2026, and new EU GmbH clients who open an account by 30 November 2026 can get a **promotional 4.20% for 4 months** on balances up to €100,000. Interest is paid daily but must be enabled in the app; when enabled, cash is held in qualifying money market funds and banks. Protection also depends on the entity: up to €20,000 under the Cyprus Investor Compensation Fund for Markets Ltd clients, while cash held with a bank for EU GmbH clients is covered by the deposit guarantee up to €100,000.

### 3. XTB — Best platform for active investors

XTB is a Polish broker listed on the Warsaw Stock Exchange since 2016, regulated by the KNF, with nearly 1.5 million active clients. Real stocks and ETFs are **commission-free up to €100,000 of monthly turnover**; above that, commission is 0.2% with a €10 minimum. It offers more than 7,000 stocks and 2,300 ETFs, fractional shares, and free Investment Plans for recurring ETF purchases. The weak point is currency conversion: **0.5%** when buying in another currency, which adds up fast on US stocks.

On cash, new clients who open an account between 14 October and 31 December 2026 get a **4.50% welcome rate on EUR up to €20,000 for 90 days** (at least one whole-share or ETF trade per calendar month required); the standard rate after that is **1.10%**. There is also a **€10 monthly inactivity fee** after 12 months without trading and 90 days without a deposit. Note that in March 2026 the KNF fined XTB PLN 20,000,000, a decision upheld on final appeal in August 2026 — worth knowing, though it does not affect the fee structure.

### 4. DEGIRO — Best for buy-and-hold ETF investors

DEGIRO, the Dutch broker now part of flatexDEGIRO Bank, is one of the cheapest ways to buy ETFs in Europe — if you stay inside its rules. Since October 2025, its **ETF Core Selection** covers more than 1,000 ETFs, ETCs and ETNs traded on the Tradegate exchange, each costing only a **€1 handling fee** per transaction, with no DEGIRO commission, no connectivity fee and no cap on the number of trades. ETFs outside the selection cost **€3** (€2 commission + €1 handling); US shares cost **€2**; European shares typically **€3.90 + €1**. A connectivity fee of **€2.50 per exchange per year** applies to exchanges other than your home market, but not to Tradegate Core Selection ETFs.

The trade-offs are real: DEGIRO pays **no interest on cash**, offers **no fractional shares** and has **no automated savings plan** — every trade is manual. It is regulated by the AFM and De Nederlandsche Bank, and uninvested money in the Cash Account is covered by the German deposit guarantee up to €100,000.

### 5. Scalable Capital — Best for larger ETF orders

Scalable Capital is a German bank and broker with over 1 million clients and more than €60 billion in client assets. On the free plan, trades cost **€0.99**; **PRIME+ costs €4.99 a month** and makes all trades of €250 or more free. Buying PRIME ETFs (Amundi, iShares, Vanguard, Xtrackers) of €250 or more is free even on the free plan, and savings plans are free from €1 on both plans. Orders on Xetra or gettex cost €1.99 on both plans.

Cash earns **2.60% a year in the separate Overnight account**, with no cap — but cash sitting in the broker account itself earns **0%**, so you must move it. Fixed-term accounts pay 2.75% for 6 months and 3% for 12 months. Scalable is only available in six countries, and its PRIME+ subscription only pays off if you trade regularly.

### 6. Freedom24 — Best for IPO access and fixed-term cash

Freedom24 is the European platform of Freedom Holding Corp., listed on Nasdaq, with its EU entity Freedom Finance Europe Ltd authorised by CySEC (licence 275/15). On the Smart plan, trades cost **€2 + €0.02 per share**. Its D-account pays daily interest linked to EURIBOR — around **2.42% on EUR in November 2026** — and fixed-term deposits pay more for locking money away: **4.29% on EUR for 12 months** (plus a 0.50% bonus in some offers). It is the only broker in this list offering retail access to selected IPOs.

The honest downside: investor compensation is capped at **€20,000** under the Cyprus scheme, far below the €100,000 deposit guarantee at bank-brokers, and per-share pricing makes it expensive for small orders of low-priced stocks.

### 7. Interactive Brokers — Best for experienced and global investors

Interactive Brokers (IBKR), founded in 1978 and listed on Nasdaq, serves European clients through Interactive Brokers Ireland Limited, regulated by the Central Bank of Ireland. Its **Tiered** pricing charges **0.05% on EU stocks and ETFs (min €1.25, max €29)** plus exchange fees, and **$0.0035 per share on US stocks (min $0.35)**; **Fixed** pricing is 0.05% with a €3 minimum, all-in. Currency conversion is the cheapest published in Europe at **0.002% on spot FX (min $2)**. Recurring ETF plans are commission-free from €10.

Interest is the weak point for smaller accounts: IBKR pays **nothing on the first €10,000 of EUR cash**, and above that roughly **1.95–2.00%** (benchmark minus 0.5%), with the full rate only on accounts with a net asset value above $100,000 — smaller accounts earn a proportionally lower rate. The platform is powerful but complex, and IBKR does not withhold tax or produce local tax statements for most European residents. Investor compensation covers 90% of losses up to €20,000.

### 8. MyInvestor — Best Spain-only option

MyInvestor is a Spanish bank (Banco de España / CNMV) combining a current account, funds, robo portfolios and a broker. Share orders cost **0.12% (min €3, cap €25)** and ETF orders **0.12% (min €1, cap €25)**, with no custody or inactivity fee. The remunerated account pays **2.50% AER for the first 6 months on up to €70,000** for accounts opened from 1 September 2026, then drops to **0.30%** unless you invest at least €300 a month in selected products, which lifts the rate back up. The Premium plan (€7.99 a month) pays the ECB rate plus 0.10 points (2.60% in September 2026) on up to €70,000. Deposits are covered by the Spanish deposit guarantee up to €100,000. The catch: it only really works if you live in Spain — the linked funding account must be at a Spanish bank.

## The Impact of Brokerage Fees on Investment Returns

Consider investing **€200 per month for 20 years** in a broad ETF with an 8% average annual return, with monthly compounding:

- **Low-cost broker (€0 custody, €0 automated plans):** final portfolio ≈ **€117,800**
- **Traditional bank broker (2% annual custody fee + €10 order fee, so €190 actually invested each month at a 6% net return):** final portfolio ≈ **€87,800**
- **Your gain from switching:** **about €30,000** kept in your pocket over 20 years

Even the custody fee alone, without any order fee, cuts the final portfolio to about €92,400. Fees are the one part of investing you can control completely — and over decades, they are the difference between a good outcome and a great one.

## How to Choose the Cheapest Broker for You

1. **Monthly ETF saver:** Trade Republic, Trading 212, Scalable Capital or XTB — all offer free savings plans. Pick on cash interest and availability in your country.
2. **Occasional stock buyer:** Trading 212 or XTB (both €0 commission up to generous limits); DEGIRO if you buy Core Selection ETFs on Tradegate.
3. **Large or frequent orders:** Scalable PRIME+ (€0 from €250) or Interactive Brokers Tiered (0.05% with a €29 cap) beat per-order fees.
4. **Cash-heavy investor:** compare interest on uninvested cash — Trade Republic (2.50% standard), Trading 212 (2.50–2.80%) and Scalable's Overnight account (2.60%) lead; DEGIRO pays 0% and IBKR pays nothing below €10,000.
5. **Protection matters:** bank-brokers (Trade Republic, Scalable, DEGIRO cash accounts, MyInvestor) cover cash up to €100,000 under deposit guarantee schemes; pure brokers (Trading 212 Markets Ltd, Freedom24, IBKR) typically cap investor compensation at €20,000.
6. **Watch the FX fee:** buying US stocks costs 0.5% in conversion at XTB, 0.25% at DEGIRO and 0.15% at Trading 212, but only 0.002% at Interactive Brokers (with a $2 minimum). On a €10,000 US order, that is the difference between €50 and about €2.

## Downsides and Honest Notes

- **Interest rates are variable and promotional.** The 4.00–4.50% headline rates are new-client offers with end dates (Trade Republic until 1 March 2027, XTB for 90 days). Standard rates track the ECB and will fall if it cuts.
- **"Commission-free" is never fully free.** Spreads, FX conversion, market-maker execution and (at XTB) inactivity fees are where the money is made. Always check the total cost of your actual trading pattern.
- **Fractional shares have limits.** Fractional holdings usually cannot be transferred to another broker — you may have to sell them if you switch.
- **Protection is not uniform.** The €100,000 deposit guarantee covers cash at bank-brokers; securities are covered by investor compensation schemes, usually capped at €20,000. Neither protects you against market losses.
- **Taxes are your job.** Most low-cost brokers do not withhold tax or file local returns for you. Keep records of every trade and dividend; our [tax guide for foreign bank accounts](/en/blog/tax-guide-foreign-bank-accounts-europe-2026) explains the reporting side.
