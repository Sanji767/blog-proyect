# N26 Review 2026: The Free Account That Is Genuinely Free — With Limits

N26 is the German digital bank that helped define European neobanking: a full bank account you open from your phone in minutes, with no branches, no paperwork and — on the Standard plan — no monthly fee at all. With [over 8 million customers across Europe](https://www.smartcreditcard.eu/en/n26-credit-card.html), it is one of the most established names in the space. But in 2026 the free account is deliberately basic, and several of the features that make N26 attractive sit behind paid plans. This review looks at what Standard actually gives you, what it costs in practice, and where N26 falls short.

## What N26 is

N26 is a fully licensed German bank — N26 Bank SE, supervised by BaFin, the Bundesbank and the European Central Bank — operating across most of the euro area under that licence. Unlike e-money wallets, your money sits in a real bank account, and deposits are [protected up to €100,000 by the German Deposit Guarantee Scheme](https://n26.com/en-eu/plans). That protection covers the main account and the Instant Savings account together.

Most customers get a **German IBAN (DE)** rather than a local one, which matters in some countries: a few employers, landlords and utilities still reject foreign IBANs for direct debits, even though IBAN discrimination is illegal under EU rules. (Spain is an exception: accounts opened there get a Spanish IBAN.) If you live in Germany, that is a non-issue. Elsewhere, it is worth checking with your employer before making N26 your salary account.

You must be 18 or over, live in one of the countries N26 serves, and own a compatible smartphone. There is no minimum income and no minimum deposit. Support is available in English, French, German, Spanish and Italian.

## N26 Standard: what the free account includes

[N26 Standard is free, with no account maintenance fees and no minimum deposit](https://n26.com/en-eu/free-bank-account). You get:

- A German IBAN and free SEPA transfers, including SEPA Instant incoming transfers at no extra cost.
- A **virtual Mastercard** usable immediately for online purchases and mobile payments via Apple Pay, Google Pay, Samsung Wallet or Garmin Pay.
- **Two free ATM withdrawals per month** in the eurozone.
- MoneyBeam, for instant free transfers between N26 customers.
- Instant transaction notifications, spending statistics and full card controls in the app — block and unblock the card, change limits, and toggle online payments and withdrawals.

The catch with the free tier is the physical card. The virtual card is free, but [a physical transparent Mastercard costs a one-time delivery fee of €10](https://n26.com/en-eu/free-bank-account). Additional virtual cards cost €2.59 each. In practice, most people who want N26 as a daily account will pay the €10 once — it is a small cost, but it means the "free" account is not quite free if you want a card in your wallet.

## Foreign currency: the 1.7% fee, explained correctly

This is the most commonly misunderstood part of N26's pricing, so it is worth being precise. **Card payments are free in any currency** — N26 applies no exchange-rate markup on card spending, in any currency and with no monthly limit. That is one of the strongest points of the free account and a genuine advantage over banks that charge 2–3% on foreign card payments.

The 1.7% fee applies to something else: [non-euro ATM withdrawals cost 1.7% on Standard and Smart](https://www.expatmoney.nl/credit-cards/n26-mastercard). So paying by card in London, New York or Tokyo costs you nothing extra, but taking cash out of an ATM outside the eurozone costs 1.7% of the amount. If you mostly pay by card when travelling, Standard is an excellent travel card. If you rely on foreign cash, the paid Go plan (€9.90/month) removes foreign ATM fees and adds travel insurance.

## The paid plans

N26 has four tiers. [Smart costs €4.90 per month, Go costs €9.90 per month, and Metal costs €16.90 per month](https://n26.com/en-eu/plans). The step-ups buy you:

- **Smart (€4.90):** a physical card included, more free ATM withdrawals, up to 10 Spaces sub-accounts, and a direct customer support hotline.
- **Go (€9.90):** free ATM withdrawals worldwide, travel insurance from Allianz Assistance, and 3 free stock/ETF trades per month.
- **Metal (€16.90):** a metal card, the highest savings rate, comprehensive insurance including mobile phone coverage and purchase protection, 10 free trades per month, and a 1% cashback promotion on qualifying transactions outside the EEA, Switzerland and the UK for up to 12 months after enrolment.

For most people, Smart is the sensible upgrade if you want Spaces and a physical card without the monthly-fee creep. Go only earns its price if you travel outside the eurozone several times a year. Metal is a lifestyle product: the insurance bundle can justify it if you would otherwise buy travel and phone insurance separately, but it is hard to justify on banking features alone.

## Spaces: sub-accounts for budgeting

Spaces are sub-accounts that sit alongside your main account, designed to organise money for specific goals — holidays, a new computer, an emergency fund. Premium customers can create up to 10 Spaces, each with its own IBAN, so you can receive SEPA transfers and set up direct debits and standing orders directly from a Space. Rules automate transfers into Spaces, Round-Ups sweep the spare change from card purchases into a Space, and Shared Spaces let you save and spend together with up to 10 other N26 users.

The limitation is that Spaces are a premium feature. Standard customers do not get the full 10-Space setup, so if envelope budgeting is your main reason for choosing N26, factor in at least the Smart plan at €4.90 a month.

## Interest on savings: very low

N26's Instant Savings account is an easy-access savings account inside the app, but the rates are among the lowest of any digital bank. [From 11 June 2025, the rates are 0.30% p.a. for Standard and Smart, 0.50% p.a. for Go, and 1.50% p.a. for Metal, before taxes](https://n26.com/en-eu/plans). On €10,000, Standard's 0.30% earns you €30 a year before tax — a fraction of what competing savings accounts pay. N26 is not the place to keep your savings; use it for spending and keep your emergency fund somewhere that pays a real rate.

N26 has also added investing: buying and selling stocks, ETFs and ETCs in the app costs [€0.90 per transaction](https://n26.com/en-eu/plans), with Go customers getting 3 free trades a month and Metal customers 10. Crypto trading is available too, with fees of 1.5% on Bitcoin and 2.5% on other coins — expensive compared with a dedicated exchange, and only worth it for small, occasional purchases.

## Honest downsides

- **Savings rates are poor.** At 0.30% on Standard, Instant Savings barely beats keeping cash under the mattress. Even Metal's 1.50% trails the best easy-access accounts.
- **The 1.7% non-euro ATM fee** makes Standard a weak choice if you regularly need foreign cash. Card payments are free, but cash is not.
- **German IBAN for everyone.** A DE IBAN can cause friction with employers, landlords or utilities in countries where local IBANs are expected.
- **Spaces are paywalled.** The budgeting feature N26 is known for requires a premium plan.
- **No branches, and support can be slow.** Everything runs through the app and chat. When something goes wrong — a frozen account, a disputed transaction — there is no branch to walk into, and account freezes, while rare, are the most common complaint about digital banks generally.
- **The physical card costs €10.** Minor, but it undercuts the "free account" pitch for anyone who wants a card in their wallet.
- **Investing and crypto are basic and pricey.** €0.90 per stock trade is reasonable, but crypto fees of 1.5–2.5% are high, and the range is far narrower than a dedicated broker.

## Who should open N26 in 2026?

N26 Standard is a good choice if you want a free, no-nonsense euro account with a German IBAN, an excellent app, and free card payments in any currency. It works well as a second account, a travel spending card, or a first account for someone moving to Germany. It is a poor choice as your only savings account — the 0.30% rate is simply too low — and a poor choice if you need a local IBAN or regularly withdraw foreign cash.

The practical setup: open Standard for free, pay the €10 for the physical card if you need one, use it for everyday spending and travel card payments, and keep your savings in a dedicated high-yield account. Upgrade to Smart only if you want Spaces and the support hotline; upgrade to Go only if you travel outside the eurozone often enough for the ATM and insurance benefits to cover €9.90 a month.
