# IBAN Discrimination in the EU: Legal Rights & How to File Complaints (2026)

When using European digital banks like **Revolut (Lithuanian LT IBAN)**, **N26 (German DE IBAN)**, or **bunq (Dutch NL IBAN)**, you might occasionally encounter an employer or utility provider claiming: *"We only accept local bank account numbers"*.

This practice is known as **IBAN Discrimination** and has been **strictly illegal across the entire European Union since 2014**.

---

## ⚖️ 1. The Legal Basis: SEPA Regulation (EU) No 260/2012

**Article 9 of Regulation (EU) No 260/2012** of the European Parliament explicitly rules that:

> *"A payee accepting a credit transfer or using a direct debit to collect funds from a payer shall not specify the Member State in which that payment account is to be situated."*

Any business or public authority operating within the SEPA zone accepting credit transfers or direct debits **is legally bound to accept any valid IBAN from all 36 SEPA member countries**.

---

## 📋 2. Step-by-Step Resolution Protocol

1. **Send Formal Notice:** Reply in writing citing Article 9 of EU Regulation 260/2012.
2. **Report to National Authorities:** File an official complaint with national financial regulators (such as Banco de España, BaFin, or ACPR).
3. **Log via Accept My IBAN:** Submit the incident to the EU-wide consumer initiative tracking compliance.
