# Best Bank Accounts for Digital Nomads in Europe 2026

A nomad's bank account has a harder job than a normal one: receive income in several currencies, pay rent in another, survive ATMs on three continents, and keep working with no fixed address. The wrong account charges you every time money crosses a border; the right one makes the border disappear.

This guide compares the five accounts that best fit nomad life in Europe in 2026: Wise, Revolut, N26, bunq and Trade Republic. Figures were checked against provider pricing pages in October–November 2026; prices vary by country, so confirm the live figure in the app first.

## Wise: the best account for getting paid in several currencies

Most nomads earn in more than one currency, and Wise is built for exactly that: hold [more than 40 currencies](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut) and get [local account details to receive money in 22 currencies](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut), including USD, GBP, EUR, AUD and SGD. A US client pays you by domestic transfer; a UK platform pays you by Faster Payments.

Every conversion uses the mid-market rate plus a fee shown upfront — [from 0.2%, and 0.46% for EUR to USD](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut) in October 2026 — with no weekend markup. No monthly fee; the physical card costs [€7 one-off](https://topewallets.com/reviews/wise). ATM withdrawals are [free up to €250 a month, then 2.69%](https://topewallets.com/reviews/wise) — the main limitation if you live on cash.

Wise is not a bank: in the EU it is a payment institution supervised by the National Bank of Belgium, and balances are safeguarded, not covered by a deposit guarantee. [Wise Interest pays 2.25% on EUR](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut) (after 0.26% annual fees, as of 23 September 2026) through a money market fund, but capital is at risk.

**Verdict:** open first if clients pay in dollars or pounds.

## Revolut: the best everyday account for moderate spending

Revolut is the strongest all-rounder for modest spenders. It is a real bank in the EU — Revolut Bank UAB, licensed by the European Central Bank — so deposits are [protected up to €100,000](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut). The free Standard plan gives you a European IBAN, lets you [hold and exchange 38 currencies](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut), and lets you [receive money from more than 160 countries](https://www.revolut.com/en-HU/money-transfer/receive-money/) with local or SWIFT details depending on the currency.

Weekday exchanges are [free up to €1,000 per month](https://www.revolut.com/en-NL/legal/standard-fees/), with a 1% fair usage fee above that; on weekends a [1% fee applies](https://help.revolut.com/en-NL/help/card-payments-withdrawals/getting-started-with-card-payments/can-i-pay-in-a-specific-currency), and crypto and commodity exchanges count towards the same limit. Premium (€10.99/month in the Netherlands) removes the cap and weekend fee and raises free ATM withdrawals to €400 a month; Metal (€19.99) raises them to €800, Ultra (€65) to €2,000.

On the free plan, ATM withdrawals are free up to [5 withdrawals or €200 per rolling month, whichever comes first](https://www.revolut.com/en-NL/legal/standard-fees/), then 2% per withdrawal (min. €1). The first card is free with a €7.99 delivery fee, and savings pay [2.50% up to €2,500, then 1.25%](https://revolut.com/en-NL/our-pricing-plans) on Standard. Tip: exchange what you need on a Friday morning, before the weekend fee starts.

**Verdict:** best free daily account under €1,000 a month of weekday exchanges.

## N26: simple euro banking with free card spending worldwide

N26 is a fully licensed German bank with a free Standard plan and a German IBAN. You hold euros only, but card payments are [free in any currency with no foreign-currency surcharge](https://banklist.co/n26) — the Mastercard rate, with no exchange limit and no weekend markup. For a nomad who earns in euros and pays by card, that covers most of life abroad.

Cash is where N26 gets expensive. Free euro ATM withdrawals are limited to [2 a month on Standard, 3 on Smart (€4.90), 5 on Go (€9.90) and 8 on Metal (€16.90)](https://banklist.co/n26), then €2 each, and withdrawals in other currencies cost [1.7% on Standard and Smart](https://banklist.co/n26). Go and Metal remove the 1.7% fee. A physical card costs €10 one-off on Standard. Spaces sub-accounts (up to 10, from Smart up) help separate rent, tax and travel money, though they share one IBAN. Savings pay [0.30% on Standard and Smart, 0.50% on Go and 1.50% on Metal](https://n26.com/en-eu/plans).

N26 is available in [24 European markets](https://bankingrating.com/blog/n26-bank-review-fees-app-account-freeze-problem/), and asks for a residential address in a country it serves when you open the account — so open it while still based in Europe.

**Verdict:** a clean euro base for card-first nomads.

## bunq: local IBANs and the best budgeting for life across borders

bunq is a Dutch bank with a full banking licence, and its killer feature for nomads is a local IBAN: [choose one from the Netherlands, Germany, France, Spain, Belgium, Italy or Ireland](https://www.bunq.com/personal/features/international-bank-accounts-and-foreign-currencies). Landlords, employers and administrations in several EU countries still reject foreign IBANs in practice; a local account number ends that argument. You can also hold money in [20 currencies](https://www.bunq.com/personal/features/international-bank-accounts-and-foreign-currencies), including USD, GBP and CHF.

Plans run from Free (€0) to Core (€3.99), Pro (€9.99) and Elite (€18.99) per month. Foreign-currency card payments cost the [Mastercard rate plus 0.5%](https://www.bankverse.ch/bunq-review/) on every plan — despite the "ZeroFX" name, bunq's own price sheet describes a 0.5% charge on top of the Mastercard rate; on Free it only covers €1,000 of spending a year, then 1.5% applies. In-app conversion costs the [Currencycloud rate plus 0.5%](https://help.bunq.com/en-fr/articles/what-are-the-foreign-currencies-accounts-fees), with an extra 0.15%–0.5% at weekends. Pro gives you 25 bank accounts, each with its own IBAN — the best budgeting setup of any European neobank.

Cash is the weak point: Free charges [€2.99 per ATM withdrawal](https://www.bankverse.ch/bunq-review/), Core €0.99 for the first five then €2.99, and only Pro and Elite include six free withdrawals a month. Receiving SWIFT payments costs €5 each — a poor choice for freelance payments from outside Europe.

**Verdict:** best second account when you settle somewhere and need a local IBAN.

## Trade Republic: the best account for cash withdrawals and interest

Trade Republic started as a broker and is now a full German bank with [more than 10 million clients in 17 European countries](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/trade-republic-review). Its appeal is simplicity: no monthly fee, a Visa debit card with [no FX fee on card payments or foreign-currency ATM withdrawals](https://quantroutine.com/brokers/trade-republic/) (Visa exchange rate), and [unlimited ATM withdrawals worldwide, free from €100](https://www.expatica.com/nl/finance/money-management/best-way-to-spend-money-abroad-when-living-in-the-netherlands-2173646/) (€1 below €100) — no monthly allowance to track. The virtual card is free; the physical Classic costs €5 one-off, the Mirror €50.

Uninvested cash earns [2.50% a year (2.53% AER) with no balance limit](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/trade-republic-review); new clients in several countries (including Spain, Portugal, Italy and the Netherlands, but not Germany) earn 4.00% on up to €20,000 until 1 March 2027. Cash is protected up to €100,000 under EU deposit guarantee schemes. Card spending earns 1% Saveback, capped at €15 a month, with at least €50 a month in savings plans.

The limitation: the cash account runs in euros only (złoty in Poland), so it cannot hold dollars or pounds like Wise or Revolut. Use it as your interest-earning cash base and ATM card, not as a multi-currency hub.

**Verdict:** best for cash abroad and the highest interest on idle cash here.

## The no-fixed-address problem

EU banks must verify who you are and where you live, and most ask for proof of address — usually a bank statement or utility bill — when you open an account. Without a home, nomads typically show [a statement from their home-country bank](https://beyondborders.plus/en/taxes). So open accounts while you have a stable address, keep one tied to your home country, and check each bank's supported countries before you move — an unsupported registered address can get the account closed.

## Tax residency: the brief, practical version

General information only, not tax advice — an hour with a cross-border tax adviser is money well spent.

The general rule is the 183-day rule: [spend 183 or more days in a country in a given year and you are generally tax resident there](https://jeangalea.com/digital-nomad-taxes/). But staying under 183 days does not make you resident nowhere — countries also weigh your home, family and economic interests, and some act sooner: Switzerland, for example, [after 30 days if you work there](https://beyondborders.plus/en/taxes).

Two consequences matter for banking. First, under the OECD's Common Reporting Standard, banks in [more than 100 jurisdictions automatically share account information](https://jeangalea.com/digital-nomad-taxes/) with tax authorities, so your accounts are reported to your declared country of tax residence. Second, [most banks will not let you open an account if you claim to have no tax residence at all](https://jeangalea.com/digital-nomad-taxes/) — you need a real, documented answer, including a tax identification number. The cleanest setup: one genuine tax residence, one bank account there, and a multi-currency account for everything else.

## The two-account setup that works for most nomads

No single account does everything. The combination that covers almost every nomad situation costs €0 a month:

1. **Wise** as your income hub: get paid in dollars, pounds or euros into local account details, convert at the mid-market rate, and spend from the balance you already hold.
2. **Revolut Standard** as your everyday account: free weekday exchanges up to €1,000 a month, a European IBAN, and deposit protection up to €100,000.

Add Trade Republic for 2.50% interest and free ATM withdrawals over €100, and bunq Pro if you settle somewhere and need a local IBAN. Always carry a second card from a different provider — a frozen account abroad with no backup is entirely avoidable.
