# Best ETF Brokers in Europe 2026: Fees, Savings Plans & Compared

Buying ETFs in Europe is cheap in 2026 — but only if you pick a broker built for how ETF investors actually invest: small, regular, automatic purchases of broad index funds, held for years. A broker with a €1 fee looks cheap until you pay it twelve times a year, while another charges nothing for the same monthly plan.

This guide compares the four brokers that matter most for ETF investors in Europe — Trade Republic, Scalable Capital, DEGIRO and Interactive Brokers — on savings plans, ETF choice, one-off trade fees, fractional investing, FX and tax reporting. Fees were checked against broker price lists and reviews updated in October–November 2026.

One thing first: as an EU retail investor you will buy UCITS ETFs, the EU-regulated fund structure. US-domiciled ETFs such as SPY or VTI are not sold to EU retail investors under the EU's PRIIPs rules — a point [Investing in the Web](https://investingintheweb.com/brokers/best-etf-brokers-in-europe/) stresses in its 2026 comparison. That rarely limits you: UCITS versions of world, S&P 500 and bond indices cover the same markets.

## What actually matters when choosing an ETF broker

**The savings plan is the deciding feature.** Most ETF investors buy monthly, not once. A free savings plan means your full €100 or €500 goes into the fund every month, apart from the market spread. A broker charging even €1 per manual order costs you €12 a year — and relies on you remembering to place it.

**The fund's own cost beats the broker's fee over time.** Every ETF charges an annual total expense ratio (TER) inside the fund — 0.07% for the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VUSA), 0.14% for the Vanguard FTSE All-World (VWCE), 0.20% for the iShares Core MSCI World (IWDA), per [Curvo](https://curvo.eu/article/degiro-costs-etf). On €10,000, the gap between a 0.07% and a 0.20% TER is €13 a year, compounding. Pick a cheap fund first, then a cheap broker.

**Currency conversion is the hidden cost.** EUR-listed UCITS ETFs on Xetra or Euronext need no conversion. But buying in dollars or pounds costs from 0.03% at Interactive Brokers to 0.25% at DEGIRO — €3 versus €25 on a €10,000 conversion, as [Brokers & Exchanges](https://brokersandexchanges.com/best/etf-brokers/) calculates.

## 1. Trade Republic — best overall for ETF savings plans

Trade Republic is the simplest low-cost way to buy ETFs every month. Savings plans in stocks, ETFs, bonds and crypto cost €0 per execution and start at €1, with over 4,900 assets available, according to [EU Personal Finance's review](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/trade-republic-review). Plans can run weekly, fortnightly, monthly or quarterly.

One-off ETF trades cost €1 with Best Price, or €2 with Direct Price if you choose the venue yourself (around 30 exchanges, including Xetra and Euronext). There is no custody, account or inactivity fee, and fractional shares let you invest from €1.

The ETF range covers more than 2,500 ETFs. Uninvested cash earns 2.50% variable interest, with a 4% offer for new clients on up to €20,000 until 1 March 2027 in several countries, per [EU Personal Finance](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/trade-republic-vs-scalable-capital). Trade Republic is a German bank supervised by BaFin, with 10 million+ clients across 18 countries. Tax handling is a genuine strength: it withholds German capital gains tax automatically, gives Dutch-branch customers pre-filled tax return data, and since 15 September 2026 its Belgian branch withholds the stock exchange tax (TOB) and related taxes ([Brokers & Exchanges](https://brokersandexchanges.com/best/etf-brokers/)). The catch: spreads under Best Price and the FX fee for US exchanges are not published.

**Best for:** automating a monthly ETF plan in one app that also handles cash and spending.

## 2. Scalable Capital — best for frequent ETF buyers

Scalable Capital matches Trade Republic on savings plans — €0 per execution from €1, on both its plans — and goes further on one-off purchases. On the FREE plan, buying ETFs from Amundi, iShares, Vanguard and Xtrackers (its "PRIME ETF" partners) costs €0 for orders of €250 or more on the European Investor Exchange (EIX). Other EIX orders cost €0.99, and orders on Xetra or gettex cost €1.99 on either plan. PRIME+ costs €4.99 a month and makes every EIX order of €250 or more free. Figures from [EU Personal Finance's Scalable review](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/scalable-capital-review), checked against Scalable's 1 September 2026 price list.

The trade-off is availability — Scalable only serves residents of Germany, Austria, the Netherlands, France, Italy and Spain. Its Overnight account pays 2.60% a year on unlimited cash. Scalable Capital Bank GmbH has held a full German banking licence since 10 September 2025, supervised by BaFin and the ECB, with over 1 million clients and €60 billion+ in client assets. On tax, it withholds German tax for German residents, while Dutch customers receive a free annual tax report prepared by KPMG. The catch: orders below €250 always cost €0.99 even with PRIME+, sells are not covered by the free PRIME ETF offer, and the currency surcharge on US dividends is not quantified.

**Best for:** investors in its six countries who buy ETFs in lump sums of €250+, or trade often enough for PRIME+ to pay off.

## 3. DEGIRO — cheapest ETF trades you place yourself

DEGIRO's draw is its ETF Core Selection: since the October 2025 overhaul, it covers all of the more than 1,000 ETFs, ETCs and ETNs traded on the Tradegate exchange, at €0 commission plus a €1 external handling fee per transaction, with no cap on trades and no connectivity fee on those listings. ETFs outside the Core Selection cost €2 plus the €1 handling fee (€3 total), and a €2.50 annual connectivity fee per exchange applies outside Tradegate (capped at 0.25% of your account value). Figures from [EU Personal Finance](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/degiro-free-etfs) and [Curvo](https://curvo.eu/article/degiro-costs-etf).

The catch is automation: DEGIRO has no native automatic ETF savings plan, so €100 a month means twelve manual orders and €12 in handling fees over a year. It pays no interest on uninvested cash, and currency conversion via AutoFX costs 0.25%. On the plus side, there is no custody, inactivity, deposit or withdrawal fee, and you get access to 45+ exchanges. DEGIRO serves 3 million+ clients in 15 European countries and is the Dutch branch of flatexDEGIRO Bank SE. For tax, Dutch customers get an annual statement for box 3 and Belgian customers get TOB withheld, but there is no tax withholding for German residents.

**Best for:** hands-on investors who place each order manually and want the cheapest per-trade cost across many exchanges.

## 4. Interactive Brokers — best for large portfolios and global ETFs

Interactive Brokers (IBKR) is the professional's choice: the widest ETF offering here, spanning 90+ market centres, with fractional shares from $1 on eligible ETFs. EU clients are served by Interactive Brokers Ireland Limited, authorised by the Central Bank of Ireland; the group has operated since 1978.

ETF pricing is percentage-based: on the Fixed plan, European ETF trades cost 0.05% with a €3 minimum (€3 on any order up to €6,000), plus a €0.75 per-execution fee on Euronext ETFs. On Tiered, the same 0.05% applies with a €1.25 minimum and a €29 cap, plus itemised exchange fees — a €500 Xetra order works out at €1.30 on Tiered versus €3.00 on Fixed, per [EU Personal Finance](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/interactive-brokers-review-europe) (17 November 2026).

For recurring investing, IBKR offers Recurring Investments in a selected range of UCITS ETFs at €0 commission from €10, while standard commissions apply outside that range, per [Brokers & Exchanges](https://brokersandexchanges.com/best/etf-brokers/). Its currency conversion is the cheapest here at 0.03%, and uninvested euro cash earns around 1.954% a year, but only above €10,000 (November 2026). The trade-offs: the platform is built for professionals and can overwhelm beginners, and there is no tax withholding in any country. The Irish Investor Compensation Scheme covers 90% of losses up to €20,000.

**Best for:** larger portfolios, investors who want ETFs from many exchanges and currencies, and anyone whose FX costs would swamp flat fees.

## The verdict

- **Best overall: Trade Republic.** Free savings plans from €1, €1 flat fee on one-off trades, 2,500+ ETFs, cash interest and the strongest tax automation, in 18 countries.
- **Best for frequent and lump-sum buyers: Scalable Capital.** Free PRIME ETF purchases from €250 on the free plan and a €4.99 PRIME+ flat rate — if you live in one of its six countries.
- **Best for hands-on trading: DEGIRO.** €1 per Core Selection trade and the widest exchange access, if you accept manual orders and no savings plan.
- **Best for large, global portfolios: Interactive Brokers.** Unmatched market access, fractional shares from $1, 0.03% FX and Tiered pricing that beats flat fees on big orders.
