# Best Bank Accounts in Spain 2026: Spanish IBAN, Bizum and Free Accounts Compared

Opening a bank account in Spain is not just about finding a free account. For newcomers, two practical details matter more than most comparison tables admit: whether you get a Spanish IBAN, and whether the account works with Bizum.

A Spanish IBAN — one starting with ES — is what employers, landlords, utility companies and public administrations expect for direct debits (*domiciliaciones*). EU rules say a non-Spanish SEPA IBAN should be accepted, but in practice many Spanish systems still reject foreign IBANs or make you argue your case. Bizum is the other filter: the instant mobile payment system built into most Spanish banks, used to split bills, pay small businesses and send money by phone number. If your bank does not support Bizum, daily life gets slightly harder every week.

This guide compares the accounts newcomers actually use in 2026: neobanks with a Spanish IBAN, traditional online accounts with no commissions, and multi-currency tools that are useful but are not a primary Spanish bank account. If you are still deciding where to settle, see our [best bank accounts for expats in Europe](/en/blog/best-bank-accounts-expats-europe-2026) and the parallel guide to [best bank accounts in Germany](/en/blog/best-bank-accounts-germany-2026).

Most newcomers end up with two accounts: one Spanish-IBAN account for salary, rent and direct debits, and one travel or multi-currency account for spending abroad. The sections below explain which combination fits which situation.

## Best all-round: N26

N26 is ranked first by [MovingToSpain](https://movingtospain.com/best-banks-in-spain/) because it combines a Spanish IBAN with low-friction onboarding. It operates under a German banking licence, so deposits are covered up to €100,000 under the German scheme. The same source notes N26 offers no credit cards or mortgages, which is why some residents later add a traditional bank.

On pricing, [Jean Galea](https://jeangalea.com/best-commission-free-banks-spain/) lists N26 Standard as free, with a free virtual Mastercard and a physical card costing €10. Paid tiers are Smart at €4.90 per month, Go at €9.90 and Metal at €16.90, with Metal including 1.30% AER on savings. The free account includes Bizum, desktop web access and an integration with Wise for transfers. [MovingToSpain](https://movingtospain.com/best-banks-in-spain/) also cites three free ATM withdrawals per month on the free account.

For most newcomers, Standard is enough: salary in, direct debits out, Bizum for daily payments. See our full [N26 review](/en/blog/n26-review-2026) for its limits.

Guides disagree on documents. [Jean Galea](https://jeangalea.com/best-commission-free-banks-spain/) describes neobanks generally as passport plus smartphone, no NIE, under 10 minutes. [How2Spanish](https://how2spanish.com/en/blog/best-neobanks-spain-relocation) states N26 requires an NIE and a Spanish address for a Spanish-IBAN account. Both may reflect different moments — onboarding rules change. Check the current in-app flow before relying on opening N26 from abroad.

## Best for spending and travel: Revolut

Revolut is strongest for day-to-day spending, travel and currency exchange. [MovingToSpain](https://movingtospain.com/best-banks-in-spain/) ranks it third, noting strong international features but less predictable acceptance in some Spanish administrative contexts. A Spanish IBAN from Revolut works for most direct debits, but some employers or administrations still prefer a traditional bank, so many residents keep Revolut as a second account.

[Jean Galea](https://jeangalea.com/best-commission-free-banks-spain/) lists Revolut Standard as free, with a Spanish IBAN, Bizum and foreign-exchange conversion up to €1,000 per month on the free plan on weekdays. Paid tiers are Plus at €3.99 per month, Premium at €9.99, Metal at €15.99 and Ultra at €55. For most people the free tier covers the core use case: spend in euros at home, exchange competitively when travelling.

Deposit protection needs care. [MovingToSpain](https://movingtospain.com/best-banks-in-spain/) describes Revolut's Spanish-IBAN deposits as covered by Lithuanian deposit insurance up to €100,000 for eligible deposits, while its summary table marks the guarantee as conditional. Protection depends on the product and where money is held. If you hold large balances, read the exact terms for your account type. Our [Revolut review](/en/blog/revolut-review-2026) explains where the free plan is enough.

## Best for multi-currency transfers: Wise

Wise is excellent at transfers, but it is not a Spanish bank account. [Jean Galea](https://jeangalea.com/best-commission-free-banks-spain/) is explicit: Wise is not a bank. It operates as Wise Europe SA under the National Bank of Belgium, customer money is safeguarded rather than deposit-insured, and it offers account details in 22 currencies at the mid-market rate. [MovingToSpain](https://movingtospain.com/best-banks-in-spain/) ranks Wise eighth as a transfers tool, not a primary bank.

Wise gives you no Spanish IBAN and no Bizum. Use it to receive salary from abroad, hold dollars or pounds, or send money home cheaply. Do not use it as the account for Spanish payroll or rent. Pair it with N26, Revolut or a traditional Spanish account.

## Best traditional backup: BBVA and Sabadell

Traditional banks still matter for mortgages, in-person problem-solving and full cash services. Spain still has roughly 18,600 branches as of 2025, according to [Freenance](https://freenance.io/fintech/best-bank-expat-spain-2026-santander-bbva-caixabank-revolut-n26-myinvestor-foreigner-kyc-comparison/), so branch access remains a real advantage outside big cities.

BBVA's Cuenta Online Sin Comisiones is the cleanest traditional option. [Jean Galea](https://jeangalea.com/best-commission-free-banks-spain/) lists it with no maintenance fee, no minimum balance and no conditions, plus English-language service. That makes it the natural backup alongside a neobank: Spanish IBAN, Bizum, branches when needed, no monthly fee to avoid.

Sabadell is the other name newcomers hear constantly. [MovingToSpain](https://movingtospain.com/best-banks-in-spain/) calls it the most expat-friendly traditional bank, with non-resident accounts and a large branch network. If you need an account before residency is sorted, Sabadell is usually the first traditional bank to try.

Watch conditional €0 offers. [Freenance](https://freenance.io/fintech/best-bank-expat-spain-2026-santander-bbva-caixabank-revolut-n26-myinvestor-foreigner-kyc-comparison/) notes Santander's Cuenta Online is only €0 with a €600 monthly direct deposit, three direct debits and card use six times per quarter. Miss one condition and fees apply. The same comparison mentions MyInvestor's Cuenta Remunerada at 2.0% on the first €30,000 — useful once resident, but not a first account for most newcomers.

## bunq: the budgeting alternative

[MovingToSpain](https://movingtospain.com/best-banks-in-spain/) ranks bunq second, noting it has added Bizum, removing its biggest weakness in Spain. It holds a Dutch licence with €100,000 protection and offers up to 25 sub-accounts, popular with people who budget by separating rent, bills, savings and spending into pots. If you want that system with a Spanish IBAN and Bizum, bunq is a legitimate primary account. If you want the simplest free account, N26 or Revolut will feel more straightforward.

Relocation guides also mention Nickel. [How2Spanish](https://how2spanish.com/en/blog/best-neobanks-spain-relocation) lists Nickel as a payment institution offering a Spanish IBAN at partner points with a passport from 190 countries. It is not a bank, so treat it as an accessibility option, not a like-for-like alternative.

## Documents: what you actually need

Traditional banks typically ask for a passport, an NIE, proof of address and proof of income, according to [Jean Galea](https://jeangalea.com/best-commission-free-banks-spain/). That is manageable once resident, but a barrier in your first weeks before an NIE appointment or rental contract.

Neobanks are built for that gap: passport plus smartphone, no NIE, under 10 minutes, per the same source. As noted, [How2Spanish](https://how2spanish.com/en/blog/best-neobanks-spain-relocation) reports N26 requires an NIE and Spanish address for a Spanish IBAN. The honest summary: some guides say passport-only, others say NIE required — check current onboarding before planning around it.

Practical sequence: open what you can before you move, get your NIE early after arrival, then add a traditional account once documents are in order. That avoids having a job offer but no account payroll will accept.

## Deposit protection in plain terms

Across the EU, bank deposits are protected up to €100,000 per person per bank. That applies clearly to N26 (German scheme), bunq (Dutch scheme) and BBVA and Sabadell (Spanish scheme), as summarised by [MovingToSpain](https://movingtospain.com/best-banks-in-spain/).

Two cases need nuance. Revolut's protection runs through the Lithuanian scheme up to €100,000 for eligible deposits and is marked conditional because coverage depends on the product. Wise, as a payment institution, safeguards customer funds — held separately from company money — but offers no deposit insurance, per [Jean Galea](https://jeangalea.com/best-commission-free-banks-spain/). For everyday balances all are reasonable; for large savings, hold money at a licensed bank with clear €100,000 coverage.

## Which setup fits whom

**Just arrived, no NIE yet.** Open whichever neobank accepts the documents you have today, use it for spending and Bizum, and add a traditional account once your NIE and address are sorted.

**Employed in Spain, salary and rent in euros.** N26 Standard or BBVA Cuenta Online Sin Comisiones as primary, plus Revolut or Wise for travel and transfers. This is the combination most long-term residents end up with.

**Remote worker paid from abroad.** Wise for receiving and converting foreign income, paired with a Spanish-IBAN account for local direct debits. Wise alone will not work for Spanish payroll or rent.

**Planning a mortgage.** Open a traditional account early — BBVA, Sabadell or Bankinter, which [MovingToSpain](https://movingtospain.com/best-banks-in-spain/) describes as suited to long-term residents with higher balances. Neobanks will not give you a Spanish mortgage.

**Budgeting by pots.** bunq, with its 25 sub-accounts, is built for exactly that.

## Fees to watch

Watch four things. Physical card fees: N26's physical card costs €10 on the free plan, per [Jean Galea](https://jeangalea.com/best-commission-free-banks-spain/). Conditional €0 offers, like Santander's €600 deposit plus direct debits and card use, per [Freenance](https://freenance.io/fintech/best-bank-expat-spain-2026-santander-bbva-caixabank-revolut-n26-myinvestor-foreigner-kyc-comparison/). Foreign-exchange limits on free tiers, such as Revolut's €1,000 monthly weekday allowance. And ATM fees once you pass your plan's free withdrawals.

The bottom line for 2026: get a Spanish IBAN and Bizum from day one, keep Wise for transfers, and add a traditional bank when you need a mortgage or a branch. That setup covers almost every newcomer situation in Spain without monthly fees you do not need.
