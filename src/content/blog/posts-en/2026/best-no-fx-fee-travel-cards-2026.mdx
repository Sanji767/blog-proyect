# Best Travel Debit Cards With No FX Fees (2026): Pay Abroad for Less

Paying with a traditional bank debit card while travelling outside the eurozone — to the United States, Japan, the UK, Thailand or Switzerland — is one of the costliest financial mistakes travellers make. Most bank debit cards add 2.75% to 3% to every payment you make abroad, on top of steep international ATM withdrawal fees. On a €2,000 trip, that is €55 to €60 gone before you buy anything. ([Source: MoneyTransfer.store, Wise Card Review](https://www.moneytransfer.store/guides/wise-card-review))

The cards in this guide cut that cost to near zero. Each one is compared on the exchange rate it actually applies, its ATM limits, and the fees that only appear in the small print — all checked against the providers' own pricing pages in November 2026.

## In-Depth Review of the Top Travel Cards

### 1. Revolut — Best for Short Trips Under €1,000 a Month

Revolut's free Standard plan is the cheapest way to spend abroad — within its limits. Weekday currency exchanges are free up to €1,000 a month, at Revolut's own rate, and you can hold and exchange 38 currencies in the app. Above the €1,000 limit, a 1% fair usage fee applies, and exchanges at weekends (Friday 5pm to Sunday 6pm New York time) carry a 1% fee on Standard. Card payments that need an exchange count towards the same limit, so a week of hotel and restaurant bills can use it up faster than you expect. ([Source: Revolut Standard fees](https://www.revolut.com/en-IT/legal/standard-fees/))

ATM withdrawals are free up to €200 or five withdrawals per rolling month, whichever comes first — five withdrawals of €20 use up the allowance just as fully as one of €200. Above that, the fee is 2% with a €1 minimum. Disposable virtual cards protect your details on international booking sites, and paid plans (Plus, Premium, Metal, Ultra) raise or remove the exchange limit and the weekend fee entirely.

**Watch out:** the weekend markup applies to card payments too, not just manual exchanges. Convert Friday afternoon's worth of local currency in the app before the weekend starts, and payments from a balance you already hold need no exchange at all.

### 2. Wise — Best for Long Trips and Large Amounts

Wise takes a different approach: it converts at the real mid-market rate and charges a visible fee on every conversion — from 0.2%, and 0.46% for EUR to USD — with no weekend markup and no monthly limit. That makes it more predictable than Revolut for big spending: €1,500 converted on a Saturday costs about €6.90 with Wise, versus €20 on Revolut Standard (1% weekend fee plus 1% fair usage above the limit). ([Source: EU Personal Finance, Wise vs Revolut](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/wise-vs-revolut))

You get local account details to receive money in 22 currencies and a card that spends in more than 40. If you hold the currency you are paying in, spending is free; otherwise Wise converts from your cheapest balance automatically. The card costs €7 one-off, with no monthly fee, and ATM withdrawals are free up to €250 a month — with no cap on the number of withdrawals since May 2026 — then 2.69% on the excess. ([Source: MoneyTransfer.store, Wise Card Review](https://www.moneytransfer.store/guides/wise-card-review))

**Watch out:** Wise is a payment institution, not a bank, so balances are safeguarded rather than deposit-guaranteed. Keep your travel money there, not your savings.

### 3. N26 — Best for Unlimited Card Payments Abroad

N26's advantage is simple: no exchange-rate markup on card payments, in any currency, with no monthly limit, on any day of the week — on every plan, including the free Standard account. Only the Mastercard exchange rate applies. For a traveller who pays by card for almost everything, that is the cleanest deal on this list. ([Source: EU Personal Finance, N26 vs Revolut](https://www.eupersonalfinance.eu/articles/n26-vs-revolut))

The weakness is cash. ATM withdrawals in foreign currencies cost 1.7% on Standard and Smart, and are free only on Go (€9.90/month) and Metal (€16.90/month). In the eurozone, Standard includes two free ATM withdrawals a month in most of Europe. If your trips involve a lot of cash — markets, taxis, rural areas — pair N26 with a second card for withdrawals, or upgrade to Go for the trip. N26 Go and Metal also include travel insurance and a 1% cashback promotion on spending outside the EEA, Switzerland and the UK.

### 4. Trade Republic — Best All-Round Free Travel Card

Trade Republic's Visa debit card is the quiet winner of this comparison. Card payments in other currencies carry no FX surcharge — conversion is at the Visa rate, free — and ATM withdrawals of €100 or more are free worldwide, with a €1 fee only on smaller withdrawals. There is no monthly fee, no exchange limit and no weekend markup. The virtual card is free; the physical Classic card costs €5 one-off. ([Source: QuantRoutine, Trade Republic Review](https://quantroutine.com/brokers/trade-republic/))

The same account pays 2.50% a year on your cash balance and earns 1% Saveback on card spending, invested into an ETF plan (capped at €15 a month, requires €50 a month in savings plans). The main drawback is practical: ten small €30 ATM withdrawals would cost €10 in €1 fees, so withdraw larger amounts less often. Card top-ups also cost 1% after the first deposit — fund the account by bank transfer instead.

### 5. Trading 212 Card — Best Free ATM Allowance

Worth a mention for cash-heavy travellers: the Trading 212 debit card allows free ATM withdrawals up to €400 a month — double Revolut's allowance — with a 1% fee above that. Card spending carries a low FX fee with no weekend markup, and uninvested cash in the account earns interest. Note that its 1.5% cashback program ends on 3 December 2026, so do not choose it for the rewards. ([Source: Investing in the Web, Trading 212 Card review](https://investingintheweb.com/brokers/trading-212-card-review/))

## How to Choose the Right Travel Card

- **Short city break, mostly card payments:** Revolut Standard — free within €1,000 a month on weekdays — or N26 Standard, with unlimited no-markup card payments.
- **Long trip or big spending:** Wise. A flat 0.46% on EUR→USD beats Revolut's 1% weekend and over-limit fees once you pass €1,000 a month.
- **Cash-heavy travel:** Trade Republic (free withdrawals of €100+) or Trading 212 (€400 a month free).
- **One card for everything:** Trade Republic — no FX fee, no limits, no weekend markup, plus interest and Saveback on the same balance.

The smartest setup is two cards: N26 or Trade Republic for card payments, plus Wise or Revolut as a backup for cash and transfers. Both combinations cost €0 a month, and a second card is also your insurance if one is lost, blocked or declined abroad.

## Critical Rule: Always Pay in the Local Currency (Avoid DCC)

When paying at a card machine abroad, the terminal may offer to charge you in your home currency (EUR) instead of the local currency. This is dynamic currency conversion (DCC) — and it is the single most expensive mistake a traveller can make at the till.

**Always choose the local currency.** The merchant's payment provider sets its own exchange rate for DCC, with a markup typically between 3% and 8%. A Norwegian Consumer Council study of 1,500 transactions found an average markup of 7.6% over the official card rate, peaking at 12.4% — and paying in local currency was cheaper in 99.7% of cases. German consumer group Stiftung Warentest measured 2.6% to 12% across 11 countries. ([Source: Navoy, What Is Dynamic Currency Conversion?](https://navoy.io/blog/dynamic-currency-conversion))

On a €1,600 trip, accepting DCC at the average 7.6% markup costs about €122, versus roughly €24 in card fees for paying in local currency. The same trap exists at ATMs — decline the conversion there too — and at online checkouts that offer to bill you in euros. Under EU Regulation 2019/518, terminals in the EU must show the DCC markup as a percentage over the ECB reference rate: if you see a number above 0%, decline it.

## Downsides and Things to Watch

- **"No FX fee" does not mean "free".** Wise charges a conversion fee instead of a markup; Revolut charges above its limit and at weekends; N26 charges 1.7% on foreign ATM cash. Read the ATM rules, not just the card-payment rules.
- **The ATM operator's fee is separate.** A "free withdrawal" from your card provider does not stop the ATM owner from charging its own fee — common in Thailand, the US and at independent machines in tourist areas. Prefer bank-owned ATMs.
- **Exchange rates are not identical.** The mid-market rate (Wise), the Mastercard rate (N26), the Visa rate (Trade Republic) and Revolut's own rate all differ slightly. On major currencies the difference is a fraction of a percent — the fees matter far more than the rate.
- **Keep a backup card.** A single card can be lost, swallowed by an ATM or blocked by a fraud check. Two free cards from different providers cost nothing and remove the risk.
